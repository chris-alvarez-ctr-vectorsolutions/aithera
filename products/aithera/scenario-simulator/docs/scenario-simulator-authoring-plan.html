Aithera · Scenario Simulator · authoring plan

Don’t catch up to their editor. Track their contract.

Six positions we are taking, six things we need back, and the mechanism that keeps the two sides aligned without another reconciliation meeting. Every claim here was read out of one of the two codebases and cites where.

The finding, dated

The dev POC contains a React port of the Writer Studio — web/src/studio/, plus web/src/styles/writer-studio.css, which is our stylesheet and says so in its own header: “extracted verbatim from writer-studio-v2.html.” Behind it sits a full authoring API.

It landed on 13 August in one squash of 194 commits (66ea6803, merged as PR #23 on the 14th) and nothing has touched any of it since — not the studio, not our ported stylesheet, not either authoring module, not the v4 schema, not the CML spec. Eleven days. Every PR since is ratings, docs, quality review and Langfuse plans.

So this is not a live tool racing ours. But it is also not one thing, and the two halves get opposite verdicts.

Ignore this half

The studio UI

A real route in their SPA (one of three in main.tsx) — but it is our design carried across so there was something to drive the endpoints with, and it has sat untouched since the day it landed.

Nothing to catch up to. Chasing a parked prototype would be the worst use of the next month, and it is the half we are best at anyway.

Track this half

The authoring API

Registered in the live app (app.include_router(authoring.router…)) with its own 422 handler for structured validation failures. Atomic writes, the same validation gate as a save, registry cache invalidation, a locked-scenario policy, three backend test suites. You do not get that as a side effect of porting a stylesheet.

Track, not adopt — and that distinction is the whole plan. We do not call it: the LMS owns storage and versioning, and a PUT here is live on the next request with no draft state and no versioning on a v4 document at all. What we track is what it reveals — the schema it validates against, the prompt it builds, the failure shape it reports. Those are the contract. The endpoints are just how they happen to expose it.

So who owns what

Our editor stays the place LXDs go. That has not changed and nothing in their repo changes it. What we are handing over is everything behind the editor — the format, the validator, the prompt builder, and eventually persistence. What stays ours is the authoring experience itself: the information architecture, the field set, the copy, the validation UX, the workflow.

Concretely, that makes our editor a lightweight in-browser editor of Scenario CML: open a file, edit it, export it, and the LMS takes it from there and owns versioning and publish. It calls nothing and depends on nothing at runtime. What we owe the format is not a connection — it is fidelity: showing every field a real document contains, validating the way their loader does, and never claiming an output is theirs when it is ours.

The end state, stated so nobody is surprised by it: when this is embedded in the Learning Object editor, the surface an LXD logs into will be built in their stack, not ours. Until then our editor is both the LXD tool and the specification for that surface. After it, the specification only. Nothing in the plan below assumes a running service, so none of it is blocked on them.

Where we actually are

Five facts, all checkable. The two that make us look worst go first.

  • We were showing an LXD about a third of a real scenario, and we found it by measuring instead of assuming. Two nested lists were bound to index 0 — a teaching topic’s points and an expert-answer group’s components. Across the eleven live documents that hid 91 teaching points and 61 expert-answer components: 152 authored fields, invisible and un-editable. They survived export, so nothing failed. Fixed and verified against marshall.lo.json.
  • On that exact problem, their port got it right and we got it wrong. SectionForm.tsx renders both lists with StringListField. Concede it early and without hedging — it is the cheapest credibility in the room, and it makes the harder positions land as engineering rather than territory.
  • Our dev-handoff export was silently dropping the one thing the 18 August meeting approved. Prior-learning-object context reached the coach in our preview and was deleted from the exported file, under a panel that said “nothing else is changed.” Now reported at export with exact paths, and flagged in Validation while authoring. It still does not reach production — ask 1.
  • The schema and the spec have been frozen since 13 August, and that explains most of what looks like disagreement. The optional neutral tier the meeting granted is in neither file — not because anyone changed their mind, but because nobody has opened either file since before the meeting happened. Read the open items that way and they get much easier to close.
  • Their format is in better shape than the conversation suggests. lo_cml_v4.schema.json is closed at the root and on all 33 $defs, the loader gates on schema_version matching ^4\., and lo_v4.py normalises a v4 document onto the same internal model the v3 builders consume — so the engine, the prompt serialisers and the goldens never see a version. Good design. Also why the drift we do have stays invisible until it bites.

Six positions

Stated as decisions, not options. Reversible if someone brings a better argument — but the default is that we do this.

Position 01

One validator, and it is theirs

We do
Stop transcribing their schema. js/scenario-v4.js is 51KB of hand-written JavaScript reproducing their schema, cross-field rules and content lint. We pin lo_cml_v4.schema.json from a named commit and validate against the file; cross-field rules stay hand-written until we can call the API, but the schema half stops being a copy.
Because
Two validators of one format drift silently by construction. It already happened. The optional neutral tier is in neither their schema (levels still requires all three) nor their spec (“All three keys are required when the block is present”) — while openingLevels already permits a partial set via anyOf, so their own two level blocks disagree with each other today. A frozen file is exactly the condition under which a hand copy quietly becomes the more current of the two.
Costs them
Nothing. It asks them to treat the schema file as the published artifact it already is, and to say when it moves.

Position 02

Our prompt is not their prompt, and the pane should say so

Done
The pane no longer claims our compiled prompt is theirs. It used to end “this exact text is what the AI runs on”; it is not — it is built by mix-arc’s prompt builder, a reimplementation of prompt_v3.build_coach_system. It now reads as the prompt the preview runs on, assembled by the editor, with production building its own from the same fields. Ours alone, and it cost nothing.
Because
Two prompt builders is worse than two validators: the difference surfaces as behaviour an LXD judges the content by, and neither side would notice it drifting. Saying so on the pane is not a fix, but it stops the tool asserting something untrue while the real fix waits for a reason to happen.
Not yet
Their POST /authoring/preview-prompt would be the real fix — it renders the actual prompt with authored-vs-template spans, and it persists nothing, so it composes fine with a file-based workflow. But it is not a plan item. It needs a reachable instance and CORS, and it 422s on any invalid document by design (“so the inspector never renders a prompt from invalid content”) while an authoring draft is invalid for nearly all of the time it is being written. Recorded as the option it is, not scheduled.
Costs them
Nothing. There is no ask here.

Position 03

Four words for two concepts, and we are picking two

We do
Propose the vocabulary and then hold it everywhere: phase is the ordered unit an author adds and reorders (author-facing: step); practice and debrief are its two halves. Stage and beat retire from anything an author or an API sees.
Because
Their spec says phase / practice / debrief, their runtime dataclass calls the turn-owning unit a Stage, resolve_active_beat calls it a beat, our editor says step, our compiled prompt says “an ORDERED SET OF BEATS.” One landmine worth naming while we are in there: lo_v4.py normalises v4 onto the v3 model, so a v4 document reports schema_family = 3 at runtime — correct, deliberate, and guaranteed to mislead the next person reading a guard clause or a log.
Costs them
A rename in the spec and in wire-facing names. Internal dataclasses can keep their history.

Position 04

An optional field is a 4.x patch, not a format negotiation

We do
Stop framing new fields as version events. Their gate is a ^4\. pattern and version.startswith("4") dispatch, so an additive optional key is compatible inside 4.x — no v5, no envelope, no migration. We ask on that basis and expect the same in return.
Because
The extension-envelope ask was the wrong ask and they were right to decline it. A namespaced must-ignore envelope means two engines reading one document and behaving differently with nothing to catch it — the exact failure the closed schema exists to prevent. Conceding that plainly is what earns the field asks a hearing.
Costs them
Nothing they are not already doing. It removes the reason to say no.

Position 05

We stop shipping extensions the format never accepted

Done
practice.answer_shape is written only where it means something. The templates pre-set it 18 times, all but two of them "open" — which is what absent already means (scenario-v4-runtime tests === 'determinate'), so the marker declared an extension, earned a stripped-extension warning and made the export lossy in order to say what saying nothing says. Those 16 are gone, and the toggle, the wizard and newPhase no longer record it. The 2 genuine determinate markers stay — and still warn, because there the field carries meaning that the format has nowhere to put.
Still open
It becomes a real field or it does not exist — those 2 are the whole remaining case, and ask 3 is what closes it. What is fixed is that a new scenario no longer opens with a warning about a default the author never chose. We had built a warning about our own default and pointed it at the LXD; whatever the eventual answer, that was the only indefensible state.
Costs them
A yes or a no on ask 3. Either is fine; silence is not.

Position 06

Both teams built the same guard, and both are blind to the same failure

Done
Field coverage is in tools/roundtrip-check.js, and it immediately found 50 more. It reads the paths the editor actually binds and matches them against every leaf the eleven production documents carry. Recorded as a baseline so only a new gap fails the run — a check that is red on day one gets muted. The same schema is now pinned under tools/pinned/, so a contract change on their side shows up as a diff in our repo.
The three worth naming: a debrief's own three quality tiers (debrief.levels.*, in 9 of 11 documents) — the same class of bug as the 152, in a second place; a character's behavior.guardrails, the lines they never cross, in 8 documents and safety-relevant; and canon_facts.reveal_when — earned disclosure, the mechanic our own ensemble type pioneered, with no field to author it.
All 50 are closed, and the baseline is empty. Every leaf in all eleven production documents now has a field that can edit it — so from here, a gap can only be one the dev team introduces or one we introduce, and either way the check says so on the next push rather than an LXD finding it.
Because
This is the finding to lead the technical part of the meeting with, because it criticises nobody. Independently, both teams built the same round-trip test against the same stated fear. Theirs is tests/test_authoring_roundtrip.py, and its docstring opens:
“The top risk of a hand-rolled JSON editor is silent lossiness: a save that looks fine in the studio but drops a field…”
Exactly right — and it cannot catch that class of bug. A byte-identical GET/PUT proves the editor did not corrupt the document. It says nothing about whether the editor could show it. That is how 152 fields hid behind a green check on our side, and the same test on their side would have passed too. Coverage is the missing half, neither of us has it, and it is the only guard that catches the next field either side adds arriving in an editor that cannot display it.
Costs them
One test and a CI job — and it is worth offering ours rather than asking: the harness is thirty lines over content they already publish. In exchange, eleven days of silent divergence stops being something either side finds out about by accident.

Six things we need back

All six are about the format. Nothing here asks them to change their service, because nothing they do to their own persistence touches a tool that never calls it. Each has a check attached, so “we’ll look at it” is visibly not an answer. Asks 1–3 block authoring work; 4–6 are cheap. Frame all of them as the first things to land next time someone opens the schema — which is true, and much easier to say yes to than a reversal.

AskWhat we want, and what proves it landed
1 · Prior context Add content.prior_context — optional object, three optional strings (title, covered, handoff), free prose, coach-facing only — as a 4.x addition. Or tell us the engine injects it from the surrounding learning object, in which case we delete the fields and stop asking LXDs to type them. Today it is approved behaviour with nowhere to live, so the scenario an LXD playtests is not the one production runs. Check: the key exists in the schema, or the injection contract is written down.
2 · Optional neutral Land it or drop it. It is in neither the schema nor the spec — because neither file has been opened since before the meeting — and opening.levels already contradicts levels on the same point. We are not relaxing our validator on a verbal decision: we would rather block an author correctly than pass content their loader rejects. Check: levels.required in lo_cml_v4.schema.json.
3 · answer_shape Adopt practice.answer_shape: "determinate" | "open", or say no and we delete it. If the ideal_response proxy is the answer, tell us what would count as passing the test you asked us to run — “evaluate the current state first” is not a testable instruction. Check: a decision with a date.
4 · Four required fields debrief.final_word, misconception, help_turns, spot_target: required because something renders them, or spec residue? Each one that is residue removes real authoring work from the remaining 61-field gap. Two specifics: help_turns is documented as defaulting to 2 and arming a mid-scene help affordance our prototype does not demonstrate at all — if it ships in V1, we need to design it, and that is on us. And the spec says spot_target “gates completion” while advancement.py never reads it. Check: four one-word answers.
5 · Turn cost Confirm the three-way split is final — clarifying question free, gibberish counts, refusal counts — and confirm the expectation that disengagement carries into coach feedback and the next phase. It needs state moving across phases; transitions[].set can carry it and nothing populates it today. We are out of line on purpose until this is confirmed, because it is one shared policy across every scenario type on our side. Check: written confirmation, then we change it.
6 · Safety — answered Closed 2026-08-25. The production engine carries always-on safety guidance in its general prompt, and authors do not control what is enabled. So the three content-safety flags are removed from our side entirely — no toggles, no extension declarations, no runtime arming, no wizard questions. Safety is a V1 product feature, not a preview feature. The unconditional learner-safety floor still compiles on every scenario; the legacy types keep their topic floors for the reference experiences the player runs.
5 · Turn cost — answered Phase 1: every learner response costs a turn, with a backlog item to revisit clarifying questions. That reverses what our notes recorded, so we are the ones diverging now: our policy rebates a clarifying question and a first “I don't know”. Ours to change — and it matters more than a cosmetic difference, because turn budgets are authored numbers: an LXD calibrating “3 turns is enough” against a preview that rebates is calibrating against an economy production does not have.

The plan

Almost none of this is a migration. It is a sequence of small moves that each leave the tool working — and the middle column needs nobody’s permission.

The two templates, and why it is not redundancy

Worth its own section because the obvious reading — “seven shapes, some overlap, trim it” — gets the fix wrong. Every template’s real output, dumped from scenario-v4-templates.js:

  • Scenario CML v4 cannot branch. At all. The schema has no branch, next, condition, goto, target or route anywhere; transition is button_label plus optional text; a phase has no successor field; and the spec never uses the word. Phases are a linear list. So “Branching Arc” produces coach_inquiry → roleplay → roleplay → roleplay — a straight four-step ladder. The name is the only branching in it.
  • “Teach Back” produces one coach_inquiry step, eight fields, under a blurb that reads “Explain it back in your own words; coverage is credited.” Nothing credits coverage on a coach step — the credited-items channel ([[spotted:]]) exists only for observe_react. The template’s own description promises the one mechanic the document it generates cannot carry.
  • The contrast that proves the point is Observe & React — also a single step, twelve fields, and completely fine, because “watch, then talk through what you noticed” is exactly what it produces, and observe_react really does credit. Step count is not the problem. Honesty is.
  • Done: one renamed, one retired. Branching Arc is now Escalating Situation — “one situation that gets harder scene by scene as the learner responds to it” — because the shape is a genuinely distinct authoring intent from Ensemble’s multi-character disclosure arc even where the mode strings look alike, and it deserves a name describing what it builds. Teach Back is out of the gallery, and that is because teach-back is a real pedagogy: the wrong move is a template quietly handing an author a plain coach chat under its name. The template object is kept in the file, so restoring it is one string once the generalized credited-items contract lands (alignment notes §9.8).
  • Six shapes now, and every label matches its output: Mix & Match (C→O→R) · Guided Arc (C→C→R) · Escalating Situation (C→R→R→R) · Ensemble (R→R→R→C) · Scene Sweep (O→C) · Observe & React (O).

Not raising, on purpose

  • Their studio UI, as a comparison. Parked since the day it landed, and the half we are best at. Bringing it up invites a conversation about whose editor is better, which is not a decision anyone needs made.
  • The eleven-day freeze, as a complaint. It is the explanation for most of the open items and it should be used that way. A frozen file is a much easier thing to unfreeze than a disagreement is to resolve.
  • The extension envelope. Declined, and correctly. Re-raising it costs credibility on ask 1, which is the one that matters.
  • Retry / rewind. They said no, and it is already gated off on our side — RETRY_ENABLED needs window.SCENARIO_ALLOW_RETRY or ?retry=1. Kept behind a flag rather than deleted, because the decision was “not now” and the early feedback was positive. Nothing to raise, and no divergence left to document.
  • The 61 remaining authoring fields. Ours. Gated on ask 4 and nothing else.
