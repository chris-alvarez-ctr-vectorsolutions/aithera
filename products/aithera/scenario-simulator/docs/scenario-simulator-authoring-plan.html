Aithera · Scenario Simulator · authoring plan

Two teams built the same tool. Time to say which one is the product.

Six positions we are taking, six things we need back, and the mechanism that keeps the two sides aligned without another reconciliation meeting. Every claim here was read out of one of the two codebases and cites where.

The thing to say first

The dev team has already ported the Writer Studio. web/src/studio/ holds a React build of it — rail, section form, inspector, prompt view, setByPath — and web/src/styles/writer-studio.css is our stylesheet, whose own header says “extracted verbatim from writer-studio-v2.html.” There is a full authoring API behind it: GET/PUT /authoring/scenarios/{id}, copy, delete, and POST /authoring/preview-prompt, which validates a draft and renders the real system prompt with authored-vs-template spans.

So “when will dev build an authoring tool” is the wrong question, and asking it is what keeps this feeling unfinished. Their tool is the product. Ours is the design spec and the LXD proving ground. Say that out loud and every remaining decision on this page follows from it.

Where we actually are

Four facts, all checkable. Two of them are ours to be embarrassed about, which is why they go first.

  • We were showing an LXD about a third of a real scenario, and we found it by measuring instead of assuming. Two nested lists were bound to index 0 — a teaching topic’s points and an expert-answer group’s components. Across the eleven live documents that hid 91 teaching points and 61 expert-answer components: 152 authored fields, invisible and un-editable. They survived export, so nothing failed — not our lints, not the round-trip check. Fixed and verified against marshall.lo.json.
  • On that exact problem, their port got it right and we got it wrong. SectionForm.tsx renders both lists with StringListField. Worth conceding early and without hedging: it is the cheapest credibility in the room, and it makes the harder positions below land as engineering rather than territory.
  • Our dev-handoff export was silently dropping the one thing the 18 August meeting approved. Prior-learning-object context reached the coach in our preview and was deleted from the exported file, under a panel that said “nothing else is changed.” Now reported at export with exact paths, and flagged in Validation while authoring. It still does not reach production — see ask 1.
  • Their format is in better shape than the conversation suggests. lo_cml_v4.schema.json is closed at the root and on all 33 $defs, the loader dispatches on schema_version matching ^4\., and lo_v4.py normalises a v4 document onto the same internal model the v3 builders consume — so the engine, the prompt serialisers and the goldens never see a version. That is a good design. It is also why the drift we do have is invisible until it bites.

Six positions

Stated as decisions, not options. Each one is reversible if someone brings a better argument — but the default is that we do this.

Position 01

One validator, and it is theirs

We do
Stop transcribing their schema. js/scenario-v4.js is 51KB of hand-written JavaScript reproducing their schema, cross-field rules and content lint. We pin lo_cml_v4.schema.json into our repo from a named commit and validate against the file. Cross-field rules stay hand-written until we can call their API — but the schema half stops being a copy.
Because
Two validators of one format drift, and the drift is silent by construction. It already exists: the meeting granted an optional neutral tier, and it is in neither their schema (levels still requires all three) nor their spec (“All three keys are required when the block is present”). Meanwhile openingLevels already permits a partial set via anyOf — so their own two level blocks disagree with each other today.
Costs them
Nothing. It asks them to treat the schema file as the published artifact it already is, and to tell us when it moves.

Position 02

One prompt builder, and it is theirs

We do
Point the Compiled prompt pane at POST /authoring/preview-prompt and render their spans[] instead of our string-matched highlighting. Until that is wired, the pane gets labelled as our approximation rather than “this exact text is what the AI runs on.”
Because
Ours is built by mix-arc’s prompt builder — a reimplementation of prompt_v3.build_coach_system. Two prompt builders is a worse problem than two validators, because the difference shows up as behaviour an LXD judges the content by. Their endpoint already validates without persisting and returns authored spans, which is exactly the pane we hand-built.
Costs them
A reachable instance and CORS. This is also the honest answer to the preview problem: an LXD previewing flow and prompt fidelity does not need our player kept in sync — it needs their prompt and a deterministic walk of the arc.

Position 03

Four words for two concepts, and we are picking two

We do
Propose the vocabulary and then hold it everywhere: phase is the ordered unit an author adds and reorders (author-facing: step); practice and debrief are its two halves. Stage and beat get retired from anything an author or an API sees.
Because
Right now their spec says phase / practice / debrief, their runtime dataclass calls the turn-owning unit a Stage, resolve_active_beat calls it a beat, our editor says step, and our compiled prompt says “an ORDERED SET OF BEATS.” One more landmine worth naming: lo_v4.py normalises a v4 document onto the v3 model, so a v4 document reports schema_family = 3 at runtime — correct, deliberate, and guaranteed to mislead the next person reading a log or a guard clause.
Costs them
A rename in the spec and in wire-facing names. Internal dataclasses can keep their history.

Position 04

An optional field is a 4.x patch, not a format negotiation

We do
Stop framing new fields as version events. Their gate is a ^4\. pattern and version.startswith("4") dispatch, so an additive optional key is compatible inside 4.x — no v5, no envelope, no migration. We will ask for fields on that basis and expect the same in return.
Because
The extension-envelope ask was the wrong ask and they were right to decline it. A namespaced must-ignore envelope means two engines reading the same document and behaving differently, with nothing to catch it — which is the exact failure the closed schema exists to prevent. Concede that plainly; it is what earns the field asks a hearing.
Costs them
Nothing they are not already doing. It removes the reason to say no.

Position 05

We stop shipping extensions the format has not accepted

We do
Remove practice.answer_shape from all seven starting templates now. It either becomes a real field or it does not exist.
Because
All seven templates pre-set it — 18 occurrences in scenario-v4-templates.js — so every new scenario opens with a stripped-extension warning per step, about a field the author never chose, and every export is lossy for no benefit. We built a warning about our own default and pointed it at the LXD. Whatever the eventual answer, the current state is the only indefensible one.
Costs them
A yes or a no on ask 3. Either is fine; silence is not.

Position 06

Alignment is a check that runs, not a meeting that recurs

We do
tools/roundtrip-check.js already fetches their live documents, replays our whole export chain and diffs the result — 11/11 green today. We add the assertion it currently cannot make, field coverage: every leaf in their documents must be reachable by an editor field. Then it runs in CI on both sides, on every push.
Because
That is the check that would have caught our 152 hidden fields on day one, and it is the check that catches the next field they add arriving in an editor that cannot show it. A round trip proves we did not corrupt the document; coverage proves we did not hide it. We need both, and neither needs a human in the loop.
Costs them
A CI job, and a stable way to fetch content and schema. In exchange, a schema change stops being something either side discovers by accident.

Six things we need back

Each one has a check attached, so “we’ll look at it” is visibly not an answer. Asks 1–3 block work; 4–6 are cheap.

AskWhat we want, and what proves it landed
1 · Prior context Add content.prior_context — optional object, three optional strings (title, covered, handoff), free prose, coach-facing only — as a 4.x addition. Or tell us the engine injects it from the surrounding learning object, in which case we delete the fields and stop asking LXDs to type it. Today it is approved behaviour with nowhere to live, so the scenario an LXD playtests is not the one production runs. Check: the key exists in the schema, or the injection contract is written down.
2 · Optional neutral Land it or drop it. It is in neither the schema nor the spec, and opening.levels already contradicts levels on the same point. We are not relaxing our validator on a verbal decision — we would rather block an author correctly than pass content their loader rejects. Check: levels.required in lo_cml_v4.schema.json.
3 · answer_shape Adopt practice.answer_shape: "determinate" | "open", or say no and we delete it. If the ideal_response proxy is the answer, tell us what would count as passing the test you asked us to run — “evaluate the current state first” is not a testable instruction. Check: a decision with a date.
4 · Four required fields debrief.final_word, misconception, help_turns, spot_target: required because something renders them, or spec residue? Every one that is residue removes real authoring work from the remaining 61-field gap. Two specifics: help_turns is documented as defaulting to 2 and arming a mid-scene help affordance, which our prototype does not demonstrate at all — if it ships in V1 we need to design it. And your spec says spot_target “gates completion,” while advancement.py never reads it. Check: four one-word answers.
5 · Turn cost Confirm the three-way split is final — clarifying question free, gibberish counts, refusal counts — and confirm the expectation that disengagement carries into coach feedback and the next phase. It needs state moving across phases; transitions[].set can carry it and nothing populates it today. We are out of line on purpose until this is confirmed, because it is one shared policy across every scenario type on our side. Check: written confirmation, then we change it.
6 · Safety, two narrow points Not reopening the flags decision. But (a) a generic trauma-informed block that routes everything to a crisis line handles adult self-harm correctly and a child-safety disclosure incorrectly — that is mandated reporting plus 911, a different obligation and a different destination; and (b) five of the six clauses in our minors provision are portrayal constraints that apply on every turn, not disclosure tripwires, so they belong in your prompt template. That needs no schema field and can land in V1. Check: the routing split, and portrayal language in the template.

The plan

Almost none of this is a migration. It is a sequence of small moves that each leave the tool working.

Done

Shipped 25 Aug

  • Nested lists editable — 152 fields recovered, verified against a production document
  • Export reports what it drops; Validation flags it while authoring
  • First run opens the front door instead of an empty form
  • Guided Arc’s vocabulary out of the rail; Guardrails → Validation, matching their API
  • Validation rows name the field the author sees; repeats collapse

Next

2–3 weeks · ours alone

  • answer_shape out of the templates
  • Pin their schema; stop transcribing it
  • Field-coverage assertion in the round-trip check, then CI
  • Prompt pane labelled as our approximation
  • Template gallery cut from seven to the shapes v4 can actually carry — Teach Back cannot be expressed in v4, and Branching Arc’s only differentiator was withdrawn by its own source deck
  • Components to core v1.22.3 / themes v1.9.3, matching what they will build against

Then

1–2 months · needs them

  • Editor calls /authoring for validation and prompt preview, behind a flag
  • Flow-only preview — a deterministic walk of steps, gates and close, needing no model and no player parity
  • Host/guest field split for the Learning Object editor: title, implementation_id, CTA label, prior context, persistence and publish are all host-owned, which is most of our top bar and Start page
  • Inspector becomes a drawer — three fixed columns do not survive being embedded
  • Export / Import demoted to a fallback once PUT is the real save

Not raising, on purpose

  • The extension envelope. Declined, and correctly. Re-raising it costs credibility on ask 1, which is the one that matters.
  • Retry / rewind as a feature request. They said no. The only live question is ours: our prototype still demonstrates it, uncapped, and a prototype that demonstrates a declined feature is actively misleading a team building from it. We will pull it unless they want it shown.
  • Whose rail information architecture is better. Theirs derives sections from the schema — one group per phase, in schema order. Ours is organised by what an author decides and when. That is a real difference and it is ours to own, but it is a design review, not an alignment item, and mixing the two is what made the last round feel like negotiation.
  • The 61 remaining authoring fields. Ours. Gated on ask 4 and nothing else.
