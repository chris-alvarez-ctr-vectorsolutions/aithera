The POC V4 conversation, on one page

The whole meeting on one page, in running order: what we found on our own side · where things stand · the four asks · the authoring walkthrough. Every ask is a change to the Scenario CML v4 format or the POC V4 engine — nothing in that section is a UX Universal decision.

If the meeting collapses to one sentence

Five of our authored scenarios, across four types, run on content their own LXD decks treat as high-risk — and handed to your engine today, every one of them loads with its safety floor silently disarmed, because v4 has nowhere to say the content is high-risk. One allowed key fixes it.

The five, and what each would lose: The Call from Home (bullying — both students are 12; its deck names the 988 Suicide & Crisis Lifeline as a closing resource) loses the minor-safeguarding and crisis floors · Reading the Warning Signs (workplace violence, escalating to a weapon) loses the threat and crisis floors, in both its branching and mix authorings · The Kendra Situation (grief and alcohol risk) loses the crisis floor, in both its authorings.

Open with this — what we found, and fixed, on our side

Not an ask. Thirty seconds at the top, because it changes how the rest lands.

  • We ran your eleven scenarios through our compiler and found we were dropping your content. 184 authored values never reached the model: every worked example (the concrete learner utterance and the reply it should draw, per tier), every character disclosure fact and its reveal condition, every roleplay emotional cue.
  • The disclosure one is the awkward one. canon_facts.reveal_when is earned disclosure — the mechanic our ensemble scenario pioneered and implements natively. We built the feature and never wired your field to it.
  • Fixed, and verified: all 184 now reach the model. Nothing about your format had to change. Worth knowing why it hid: our compiler was validated by comparing its output against our own older scenario types, and that test is blind to any field you have that we don't — which was exactly this set.

Where things stand — the two-minute update

All settled facts, nothing to decide. The point of saying it: they should hear that v4 is not an experiment on our side any more.

  • We adopted your format, end to end. Authoring and play both run on Scenario CML v4 — the typeless document, the fixed unthoughtful / neutral / strong scale (our custom tier names are retired), the phase/practice/debrief model, the content lint. Our seven scenario types survive as authoring templates, not as a field anything reads.
  • One player runs all of it. A single ?type=-routed player over one shared engine (scenario-live.html + js/sim-player.js); v4 documents reach it through one compiler (js/scenario-v4-runtime.js). There is no per-type runtime left to keep in sync.
  • The three engine changes we owed you are shipped and verified. Debrief as its own rung, per-scope prompt compilation, verbatim carryover — all landed and confirmed against the spec (notes §5).
  • Two teams, no comparing notes, the same structures. Our converter’s output independently matched your own ports of the same source decks — ensemble ↔ bullying (RRRC), scene-sweep ↔ hazcom (OC), guided-arc ↔ marshall (CCR). That is the strongest evidence the model is right.
  • What is left is ours and it is finite. 61 authoring fields still unfilled (128 before our mechanical defaults). Flipping v4 from an opt-in (?type=v4-universal) to the default authored source is gated on those, plus the four asks below — not on any missing engine work.

The four asks

Asks 1 and 2 are two separate decisions that happen to share one small schema change — answer them independently; a no on 2 should not sink 1. Ask 1 is the one that matters. Asks 3 and 4 are small and free-standing. Each carries where it came from, because “an LXD deck requires it” and “we would like it” are very different asks and should not sound alike.

01

Can a scenario declare that it is high-risk?

Question
Can a document say this content is crisis-adjacent / involves minors / carries threat content, so an engine can arm the matching behavior?
Why
These flags make a coach drop the exercise when a learner discloses real distress mid-scene, and surface crisis support. Runtime behavior, not wording.
Where it
came from
Your bullying scenario shows the gap exactly. It puts “988 Suicide & Crisis Lifeline” in closing.ideal_response.source_references — a citation on the results screen. That is not a floor that can fire during the conversation, and no field could trigger one. The deck asks for the resource; the format can only footnote it.
Proof
Four of your scenarios carry high-risk content — bullying (two 12-year-olds), wpv (a weapon), kendra, renee — and none of the eleven can declare it. Five of ours set a flag; hand them over today and the floor silently doesn't arm.
If yes
Three booleans — elevated_stakes, threat_content, involves_minors.
02

Can a practice declare whether it has a right answer?

Question
Can a practice say this has a correct conclusion versus this is open judgment, in a way a machine can read?
Why
It decides whether the coach lands a verdict or deepens the learner's own answer. Wrong on a reflection beat, the coach grades what was meant to be explored.
Where it
came from
Your content spans both; the format can't tell them apart. marshall asks the learner to commit to whether this qualifies as sexual harassment — right answer. kendra asks them to stay with her through her pushback — no right answer, only better and worse. Both are just an exit.requirement, which says what the learner must do, not whether a conclusion is correct.
Proof
We tried inferring it from the exit requirement: wrong on 13 of 14 beats. levels.strong.look_for carries it to a human reader, not to a parser.
Caveat
Quality, not safety — lower stakes than ask 1, and reasonable to defer while saying yes to the flags. That's why they're separate questions.
Shared note — both of the above need the same one-line change

However those two land, the mechanism is the same and it is small: one extensions object on content and on each practice, namespaced keys (vector:*), under the rule that an engine must load what it does not implement and must ignore it. Measured on your Marshall: flat, the loader rejects at one path per field, scattered; enveloped, every rejection is the same single key.

The objection, answered: “additionalProperties:false is deliberate.” It still is — a typo'd final_wrod still fails loudly, there is one sanctioned place vendor data may live, and your lint applies inside it. Fallback: adopt whichever fields you accept into the schema directly. Either works for us; we author flat and fold only on export.

03

Stop requiring fields no author ever wrote

Ask
Make purpose optional (or give it the prompt consumer the spec describes), and agree final_word is never invented to fill a slot.
Why
Provenance, not effort. A required field with no reader gets filled with something — and that something ends up in shipped compliance content.
Where it
came from
All three LXD decks, searched. The fields v4 requires and no engine reads are absent everywhere; the fields that carry actual teaching are everywhere. That contrast is the argument.
Across the workplace-violence, bullying and HazCom decks — purpose 0 / 0 / 0 · final_word 0 / 0 / 0 · misconception 0 / 0 / 0. Against “what to look for” 12 / 12 / 9 and “debrief” 32 / 28 / 30. (rubric is the honest exception: 0 / 1 / 10 — HazCom really does author one, which is why observe scenarios are the one place we can source it.)
Proof
Your own two documents, quoted exactly. The alignment audit calls purpose “the only required spec field with a stated prompt consumer that has none.” The punch list logs a possible invented teaching point — “no counterpart anywhere in the 15-slide deck. Compliance-relevant field” — still open pending your full deck audit. We cite it as the question a reader-less required field creates, not as a proven defect.
Pushback
“They're cheap to write.” That's how the invented compliance claim got in. The cost isn't the typing.
Note
Not blocking us — we already default these. The ask is to make it shared policy rather than a one-sided workaround.
04

A clarifying question shouldn't cost a turn

Ask
On coach practices, let an off-script or clarifying learner turn re-ask without spending budget.
Why
On a 2-turn budget one confused question burns half the learner's chance to actually practice — and 2 is the modal cap in your own content (the WPV and HazCom decks author caps of 2 and 3 throughout).
Where it
came from
Us, not a deck — worth saying plainly. “Clarify” appears zero times in all three decks; no LXD ever contemplated this. We hit it in testing, watched learners spend a scarce turn asking what was being asked, and fixed it. It is a learner-experience argument, not a requirement anyone wrote down.
Proof
Shipped and running on every scenario of ours, in two halves: the policy the model is given is SimCore.nonAnswerPolicy (js/sim-core.js, compiled in by all five ladder types), and the turn-count rebate itself is one line in the player — if (counted && turn.action === 'redirect') state.turnsInPhase--. Reference implementation available to diff.
Pushback
“The cap is derived from budgets — rebates complicate it.” It's budget-inert by the same logic as your own coach-help turns (§7.2.4), which are already exempt from the cap.

The authoring walkthrough — what works today, and what is gated

No decision needed here either. Best given with the Editor open — the running order below is the tool’s own order.

  • Universal Scenario is the go-forward editor, and it authors your format directly. Not a UX-Universal document converted on the way out — the writer is editing Scenario CML v4 itself. The rail is the document: Basics → Situation & world → Coach voice → Teaching points → Opening reflection → Steps → Expert answer → Dev handoff, with the engine guardrails visible but locked.
  • A writer starts from one of seven v4 templates — the old scenario types, in their new job. Picking one fills a complete, valid document they then edit; nothing downstream can tell which template it came from.
  • Dev handoff exports the file you would load. Two profiles from the same draft: <id>.lo.json, which validates against your schema today and lists what it lost on the way out, and <id>.proposed.lo.json, the same document with the envelope from ask 1. This is where ask 1 stops being abstract — the losses are printed on screen.
  • Draft → playtest → publish, without leaving the tool. A writer runs the draft against the live model before publishing anything; publishing hands the same draft to the player.
  • The classic shapes are now marked Legacy — for editing existing scenarios. The six still in the picker — Roleplay, Guided Arc, Teach-Back, Scene Sweep, Ensemble and Mix & Match — stay fully editable so existing content keeps a home, but new work is steered to Universal Scenario. Worth saying plainly: we are not asking anyone to maintain seven authoring paths.
  • Gated — guided setup for Universal Scenario. The interview-to-first-draft flow (a short brief, a few questions, a complete draft) exists for the legacy shapes and not yet for v4; picking Universal Scenario opens the editor on its template gallery instead. This is our work, not blocked on you.
  • Gated — v4 as the default rather than an opt-in. The Editor already steers new work there; what is left is dropping ?type=v4-universal from the URL, and that waits on the four asks and the 61 fields. No engine work is in the way.
  • Prototype scaffolding, so it is not mistaken for architecture. Drafts live in localStorage and the model calls go through a shared dev proxy. Multi-user persistence and a productionized worker are the two pieces production replaces — both behind seams that already exist.
