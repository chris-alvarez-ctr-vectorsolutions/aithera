The POC V4 conversation, on one page

Three asks, in the order to raise them. Each one is a change to the Scenario CML v4 format or the POC V4 engine — nothing here is a UX Universal decision. Open with what we found on our own side first; then the asks, then what to park and what to leave out.

If the meeting collapses to one sentence

Five of our authored scenarios, across four types, run on content their own LXD decks treat as high-risk — and handed to your engine today, every one of them loads with its safety floor silently disarmed, because v4 has nowhere to say the content is high-risk. One allowed key fixes it.

The five, and what each would lose: The Call from Home (bullying — both students are 12; its deck names the 988 Suicide & Crisis Lifeline as a closing resource) loses the minor-safeguarding and crisis floors · Reading the Warning Signs (workplace violence, escalating to a weapon) loses the threat and crisis floors, in both its branching and mix authorings · The Kendra Situation (grief and alcohol risk) loses the crisis floor, in both its authorings.

Open with this — what we found, and fixed, on our side

Not an ask. Thirty seconds at the top, because it changes how the rest lands.

  • We ran your eleven scenarios through our compiler and found we were dropping your content. 184 authored values never reached the model: every worked example (the concrete learner utterance and the reply it should draw, per tier), every character disclosure fact and its reveal condition, every roleplay emotional cue.
  • The disclosure one is the awkward one. canon_facts.reveal_when is earned disclosure — the mechanic our ensemble scenario pioneered and implements natively. We built the feature and never wired your field to it.
  • Fixed, and verified: all 184 now reach the model. Nothing about your format had to change. Worth knowing why it hid: our compiler was validated by comparing its output against our own older scenario types, and that test is blind to any field you have that we don't — which was exactly this set.

The three asks

Ask 1 is the one that matters. Asks 2 and 3 are small and independent — either can be dropped without affecting the others. Each carries where it came from, because “an LXD deck requires it” and “we would like it” are very different asks and should not sound alike.

01

Allow one extensions key

Ask
One extensions object on content and on each practice, namespaced keys (vector:*), under the rule that an engine must load what it does not implement and must ignore it.
Why
We author four fields v4 can't express — three safety flags (crisis, threat, minors) and a graded-vs-open marker. Today the only way to hand a scenario over is to delete them.
Where it
came from
The decks require the behavior; v4 has no field to carry it. The bullying deck specifies the closing resources outright — “988 Suicide & Crisis Lifeline · StopBullying.gov · your district's anti-bullying policy” — on a scenario whose two characters are 12 years old. The WPV deck names EAP 15 times and threat 32 times. The flags are how that authored intent reaches the runtime.
Proof
Not one scenario — five authored instances across four types carry at least one flag. Strip them and nothing in the document records that a floor was ever meant to exist.
Pushback
“additionalProperties:false is deliberate — unknown fields are usually typos.” It still is: a typo'd final_wrod still fails loudly. There is exactly one sanctioned place vendor data may live, and your content lint applies inside it.
Fallback
Adopt the four fields into the schema directly instead. Either shape works for us — we author flat and fold only on export.
02

Stop requiring fields no author ever wrote

Ask
Make purpose optional (or give it the prompt consumer the spec describes), and agree final_word is never invented to fill a slot.
Why
Provenance, not effort. A required field with no reader gets filled with something — and that something ends up in shipped compliance content.
Where it
came from
All three LXD decks, searched. The fields v4 requires and no engine reads are absent everywhere; the fields that carry actual teaching are everywhere. That contrast is the argument.
Across the workplace-violence, bullying and HazCom decks — purpose 0 / 0 / 0 · final_word 0 / 0 / 0 · misconception 0 / 0 / 0. Against “what to look for” 12 / 12 / 9 and “debrief” 32 / 28 / 30. (rubric is the honest exception: 0 / 1 / 10 — HazCom really does author one, which is why observe scenarios are the one place we can source it.)
Proof
Your own two documents, quoted exactly. The alignment audit calls purpose “the only required spec field with a stated prompt consumer that has none.” The punch list logs a possible invented teaching point — “no counterpart anywhere in the 15-slide deck. Compliance-relevant field” — still open pending your full deck audit. We cite it as the question a reader-less required field creates, not as a proven defect.
Pushback
“They're cheap to write.” That's how the invented compliance claim got in. The cost isn't the typing.
Note
Not blocking us — we already default these. The ask is to make it shared policy rather than a one-sided workaround.
03

A clarifying question shouldn't cost a turn

Ask
On coach practices, let an off-script or clarifying learner turn re-ask without spending budget.
Why
On a 2-turn budget one confused question burns half the learner's chance to actually practice — and 2 is the modal cap in your own content (the WPV and HazCom decks author caps of 2 and 3 throughout).
Where it
came from
Us, not a deck — worth saying plainly. “Clarify” appears zero times in all three decks; no LXD ever contemplated this. We hit it in testing, watched learners spend a scarce turn asking what was being asked, and fixed it. It is a learner-experience argument, not a requirement anyone wrote down.
Proof
Shipped and running on every scenario of ours, in two halves: the policy the model is given is SimCore.nonAnswerPolicy (js/sim-core.js, compiled in by all five ladder types), and the turn-count rebate itself is one line in the player — if (counted && turn.action === 'redirect') state.turnsInPhase--. Reference implementation available to diff.
Pushback
“The cap is derived from budgets — rebates complicate it.” It's budget-inert by the same logic as your own coach-help turns (§7.2.4), which are already exempt from the cap.

Park — mention, don't open

Say the sentence, get an acknowledgement, move on. None of these needs resolving in this meeting.

  • Retry / mastery. The Kendra deck asked for it and your punch list already logs dropping it as a deviation from authored intent. Neither engine has it, so nothing regresses — just keep it tracked.
  • Pluggable interaction surfaces. Generalizing the [[spotted:]] credited-items contract beyond observe_react. Real architecture work with a real design behind it — book it separately or it eats the whole hour.
  • Two defaults to confirm. help_turns defaults to 2, quietly arming the mid-scene help affordance for any scene interaction that omits it — while all six of your SME scenarios author 0 and every 2 sits in a generated demo. And spot_target doesn't gate completion in the build, though the spec says it does.

For their awareness — no decision from us

Worth naming once, because alignment targets the specification and these are places the spec and the shipped engine disagree. Theirs to reconcile.

  • Five spec-versus-build divergences inside POC V4 — forward-only advancement versus the retry edge in next_phase_id; coach steps advancing on reply shape rather than exit.requirement; spot_target not gating; teaching rendering into the opening prompt; and an optional learner-facing score in the closure block. Detail: spine alignment §E.

Leave these out

Listed so the omissions are deliberate rather than forgotten — each one is either ours to decide, already settled, or withdrawn.

  • Everything we decided for our own stack — the authored format, the runtime strategy, adopting your three-level scale, our conversion policy, our templates, our defaults. Recorded for transparency; not on the agenda.
  • Our three shipped engine changes — debrief rung, prompt scoping, carryover. Done, verified, ours.
  • Our remaining authoring backlog — the 61 unfilled fields. Our work, not a discussion item.
  • A partial two-level quality scale. We were going to ask for this. Then we counted: all three decks author all three tiers, and abundantly — bullying 14 / 14 / 16, WPV 8 / 11 / 15, HazCom 6 / 6 / 12. Five of our seventeen ported practices carry only two poles, and that is our porting artifact, not a limit in the source. Ours to finish authoring, not a v4 change.
  • Tier-routed branching. Its own source deck withdrew the requirement, and no scenario ever forked on it. Raising it argues for a feature nobody asked for.
  • Watch-and-discuss. We claimed this as a gap; we were wrong. coachInteraction.media accepts video and is ungraded — that is watch-and-discuss.
  • Narrative placement and teaching grouped by subject. The first is resolved on our side; the second we've accepted as-is. Neither is worth a slot.
