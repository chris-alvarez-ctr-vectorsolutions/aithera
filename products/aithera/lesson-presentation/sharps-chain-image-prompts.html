Chain image prompts

Twenty-four fully assembled prompts for the “One Decision” beat in Contain the Sharp — six moments across each of the four sectors. Every block below is complete and ready to paste: the locked style and constraint text is already baked into each one, so nothing needs joining together.

Uncatalogued working doc · sibling to sharps-module-flow.html

Public Sector Manufacturing Education Commercial AEC Style reference Notes
Two things to get right or the set is unusable. Faces: every camera line specifies the framing that excludes them rather than saying “no faces” — a negative prompt reliably produces a mangled one. The injury: it is the pivot of the beat and it is written to be implied, not shown. No blood, no wound, no needle entering skin — the contact and the flinch carry it.
Moments 2 and 3 are alternatives, not a sequence. The learner picks a route, so one run shows the container act or the shortcut act — never both. Generate both; the beat serves whichever they chose.

Public Sector

EMS · a broken ampoule in the back of a rig

1 · Setup 2:10 AM

The sharp is in hand and both destinations are visible in one frame. The whole decision has to be readable without a caption.

Full prompt — Public Sector · Setup 
Desaturated documentary photograph. Near-black blue-grey environment, deep
shadow holding most of the frame. One cold practical light source in shot
(fluorescent tube, dome light, work lamp) throwing a narrow teal-cyan pool;
everything outside it falls to near-black. Anamorphic 40mm feel, shallow depth
of field, focus locked on the object of the action. Fine 35mm grain, muted
palette, no colour outside the teal-cyan light and one warm amber accent
where a hazard sits. Restrained and unglamorous. Realistic workplace surfaces
with wear on them. Editorial, not stock.

SCENE: The cramped rear compartment of a parked ambulance at night. A broken glass ampoule held between gloved fingers over a drug-box tray. A red wall-mounted sharps container behind the bench seat, further in. An open trash bag hanging from the equipment rail at knee height, immediate and close.

CAMERA: Over-the-shoulder from behind, the subject's head out of frame above the top edge. The sharp in the near hand, sharp focus. Both destinations visible in the same frame — the correct container deeper in and softly out of focus; the wrong receptacle close and immediate.

Framing: the figure is never the subject. Crop below the collarbone, shoot from
behind, or turn the body fully away from camera. Hands, forearms and the object
carry the whole story. No eye contact, no recognisable face, no portrait
framing at any distance.

Do not render: blood, wounds, punctures entering skin, medical gore, visible
injury detail, brand names, readable text, logos, watermarks, signage copy.

Keep it plausible: real protective equipment worn correctly for the trade,
correct-scale tools, no decorative sparks or lens flare, no dramatic sky, no
heroic posing. This is a shift, not an emergency.

If the photographic look fights the page

The beat sits on teal line-art SVG figures. If twenty-four photographs read as too heavy beside those, swap the opening paragraph of the style block for this and keep everything else identical — the camera and constraint lines still do their job on an illustration.

no rendered lighting. Geometric, confident line, generous negative space.

Notes on using these

  • Generate the setup frame first, per sector. It establishes the location, light and wear. Once one looks right, feed it back as a reference image for the other five so the space stays the same room.
  • Moment 4 is the one that will fight you. Generators want to centre and light a person. If a face creeps in, push the figure further into the frame and add distance rather than adding negatives.
  • Moment 5 may get refused depending on the tool. If so, drop the word “flinch” and describe only the bag against the forearm and the open hand — the timeline text already names what happened, so the image only has to hold the instant.
  • Generate at 3:2, display at about 16:9. The timeline stacks five nodes, so a tall image makes the screen enormous. Generating wide gives you crop room.
