None of them is an internal UX Universal choice dressed up as a proposal — the decisions we made for our own stack (the authored format, the runtime strategy, adopting your three-level scale, our conversion policy) are recorded in the alignment notes under §4 and §14, and they are not on this agenda. Context: what UX Universal has already adopted context

Before any proposal: UX Universal took the V4 format as its source of truth. No scenario type field (the scenario types became authoring templates — seven of them ship in the Editor), the fixed unthoughtful / neutral / strong scale (the custom tiers retired), the phase model, the content lint. The converter's output independently matched the POC team's own ports of the same source decks — ensemble ↔ bullying (RRRC), scene-sweep ↔ hazcom (OC), guided-arc ↔ marshall (CCR). Two teams, no comparing notes, same structures. UX Universal moved to the V4 format on nearly every point; the proposals below are the remainder, where the evidence suggests the format or engine should move.

Part 1 · The proposals

Five proposals, in the order to raise them

#1 Extension envelopes — one key in the schema first up

Proposal
Allow one property, extensions, on content and on each practice — namespaced keys (vector:*), under the rule an engine MUST load what it does not implement and MUST ignore it. Full writeup with the before/after: the extensions proposal.
Evidence
UX Universal scenarios carry four fields V4 cannot express — answer_shape (right-answer vs open-reflection step) and three safety flags (elevated_stakes, threat_content, involves_minors). Today the only handoff option is deleting them. Measured on your Marshall with the extension values authored in: flat, the loader rejects at one path per field, scattered through the document (6 with all three flags set, 4 with only the flag Marshall needs); enveloped, every rejection is the same single key kind — extensions — whatever you author. That invariant, not the raw count, is the size of the schema change.
Why it matters
Without the flags, a workplace-violence scenario loads in the V4 engine with no crisis floor and no trace one ever existed. Not a style difference — a silent safety regression.
Likely counterpoint
"additionalProperties:false is deliberate — unknown fields are usually typos." The instinct is right, and the envelope preserves it: a typo'd final_wrod still fails loudly, there is exactly one sanctioned place vendor data may live, and the §9.2 content lint applies inside it. On answer_shape specifically, the likely counterpoint is "that's what levels.strong.look_for encodes" — true for a human reader, not machine-readable.
Fallback
Direct field adoption instead of the envelope (the four fields join the schema proper). That costs a schema change per future field, but either form works for UX Universal — authoring keeps flat fields internally, and folding is only an export projection. If either form is accepted, the schema diff and test fixtures are ready to hand over the same day.

#2 Mechanical fields: default them, don't hand-author them

Proposal
For fields the engine requires but no SME ever wrote — purpose on every phase and practice, the two transition.button_labels, final_word — agree they are engine defaults or generated at export, never hand-authored SME content.
Evidence — the decks never contained them
Searched the final 36-page WPV deck: purpose ×0, final_word ×0, misconception ×0, rubric ×0. The POC's own punch list documents what filling required slots did:
"several locked final_word lines across the LOs are authored (spec-required) with no deck antecedent" · "likely filled to satisfy a schema slot, not SME-authored" · "Possible invented teaching point… no counterpart anywhere in the 15-slide deck. Compliance-relevant field."— docs/authoring/sme-punch-list.md
And on purpose specifically, the POC's own alignment audit (item P5) found it is never rendered into any prompt — "the only required spec field with a stated prompt consumer that has none." A required field with no consumer accounts for 36 of the 128 required-field gaps across the seven ported scenarios — the single largest category. UX Universal has already defaulted all 36 with generated stub prose, which unblocks our authoring but does not answer whether the field should be required; that call is yours.
Likely counterpoint
"The fields are cheap to write." The Marshall flag answers it — carefully, in your own words: a teaching point on a compliance-relevant field that your own traceability pass logged as a possible invention with "no counterpart anywhere in the 15-slide deck," still open pending the full deck audit. We are not claiming it is confirmed. The point is that a required field with no reader is how that question arises at all — the issue isn't effort, it's provenance.
Fallback
UX Universal already ships house defaults (measured from the 11 POC scenarios) that take our own blocking count from 128 to 61, so nothing is blocked either way; the proposal makes the policy shared rather than one-sided.

#3 Retry — the decks asked for it; make it an option

Proposal
A per-step "may retry" flag. Forward-only advancement stays the default everywhere the flag is absent — this adds an option, not a rewrite.
Evidence — already an open item on the POC side
"can trigger another scene progression to retry or play out further" · "the scenario continues until you see what works"— the Kendra deck (authored LXD intent)
"Recorded as a real deviation from authored deck intent; decide whether a bounded retry/mastery feature belongs on the roadmap."— sme-punch-list.md
The punch list already logs dropping retry as a deviation. This proposal seconds that open item, with a shape (optional, per-step) that preserves the forward-only architecture.
Likely counterpoint
"Forward-only is a deliberate guarantee (derived caps, guaranteed close)." The per-step flag answers it: a bounded retry re-enters the same practice with the same budget rules; the cap and the close survive.
Fallback
Roadmap acknowledgment. Neither player has retry today, so nothing regresses while it waits — and the punch list already records it as authored intent, so an acknowledgment keeps it tracked.

#4 House conventions — two small decisions the content already needs

Proposal (a)
Pick the practice-button convention. Measured on the six SME scenarios — the right evidence base for an authoring convention, since GENERATED-DEMOS.md marks the other five as Claude-authored spec exercisers: the practice→debrief button is authored 17 times and 16 are the identical string "Talk it through" (2 distinct in all). That is not drift, it is an unwritten default; write it down. The debrief→next button is the opposite — 10 distinct across 17, story-carrying labels ("Sit down with Bianca", "Find Marco") — leave that one authored. Across all 11 the shape is identical, just diluted by the demos: 29 authored / 7 distinct / 23 identical. Related finding: "Begin practicing" is used as both an opening and a debrief label. The punch list already flags the sibling issue ("Last-debrief transition text splits 3-3 across the family… SME/LED to pick the house convention").
Proposal (b)
Withdrawn 2026-08-18 — kept here so it is not re-raised. This was going to ask for a partial quality scale on practices, on the premise that authored beats usually ground only two tiers. Measurement says otherwise: all three LXD decks author all three tiers, and abundantly — bullying 14 / 14 / 16, WPV 8 / 11 / 15, HazCom 6 / 6 / 12 by tier label. Five of our seventeen ported practices carry only two poles, which is our porting artifact rather than a limit in the source. Ours to finish; nothing to ask for.
Likely counterpoint
On (b): "the classifier needs all three." The opening's levels are already partial by design, so the engine handles it — the open question is why practices are different.

#5 A clarifying question shouldn't cost a turn

Proposal
Adopt the redirect rebate for coach steps: an off-script or clarifying learner turn re-asks without burning budget.
Evidence
The V4 spec is explicit the other way — for a coach practice, "a clarifying question is just a turn." On a 2-turn budget (the common case in the POC content), one confused question burns half the learner's chance to actually practice. UX Universal hit exactly this and fixed it, in two halves: the model-facing policy is SimCore.nonAnswerPolicy (js/sim-core.js), compiled in by all five ladder types, and the turn-count rebate is one line in the player — if (counted && turn.action === 'redirect') state.turnsInPhase--. Every UX Universal scenario runs on it; a reference implementation is available to diff against.
Likely counterpoint
"The cap is derived from budgets — rebates complicate it." The rebate is budget-inert by the same logic as V4's own coach-help turns (§7.2.4), which are already exempt from the cap.
Fallback
None required — this is engine behavior on the V4 side. If declined, the difference stays on record as a documented learner-experience gap between the two players (notes §9.2).
Part 2 · The architecture session

#6 — Generalize [[spotted:]] into a pluggable interaction layer separate meeting

Terminology note
The word "extension" is doing different work here than in #1. #1 proposes schema fields added to a document. This point proposes pluggable interaction surfaces in the engine — modules, not fields. A surface is not an extension, and neither requires the other (notes §9.8 and the Terminology table).
Proposal
V4's [[spotted:]] crediting contract — the engine strips the marker, validates ids against the rubric, "the player's meter reads only this" — is exactly the right pattern: the engine consumes typed outputs and stays agnostic to the interaction. But it exists for observe_react only. Generalize it (credited items independent of what the learner is looking at) and treat the interaction surface as pluggable, the way the format's oneOf already treats the interaction shape.
Evidence — the growth rule covers the format, not the engine
The V4 spec: "a new practice type is one new mode value plus one new interaction shape. The spine never changes." The rule holds for the schema; the docs state no engine-side equivalent, so today a new mode implies engine work. The cost is already visible: teach-back (retrieval) is inexpressible in V4 precisely because the one output contract is tied to observe's required exhibit.
The working demonstration
UX Universal runs the same pattern generalized. A surface registers into SimSurfaces keyed by phase kind and declares typed turnFields outputs with validators; the engine parses the model's turn, validates the ids, and hands the credited values back. Spot the Hazard has two interchangeable surfaces — the photo/hotspot canvas (js/sim-perception.js) and the text-observation log (js/sim-observe-text.js) — same authored scenario, same output contract, swapped by a URL flag with zero engine edits. Teach-back (js/sim-teachback.js) goes further and owns the composer via ownsInput. Three interactions, no runner surgery.
Why it's a separate session
It's an architecture discussion with real design work behind it, and bundling it with #1–#5 risks crowding them out. Raise it only if the conversation invites it, or book it separately.
Part 3 · Flag in passing

Small items — mention, don't debate

ItemWhat to say
help_turns default The engine default (DEFAULT_HELP_TURNS = 2) silently turns the mid-scene help affordance ON for any scene interaction that omits the field. The split in your own content is the argument: all six SME scenarios author 0 (nine instances across bullying / hazcom / kendra / marshall / renee / wpv); every 2 sits in a Claude-generated demo (customer-deesc, floor-lead, prep-line). No SME-authored scenario wants it on. Suggest defaulting OFF.
Spec/schema disagreement on coach media The spec prose says a coach step's pinned media is a "reference image"; the schema allows video. Small, but it's the kind of drift the POC's own alignment audit tracks — and the video reading matters (it's how watch-and-discuss maps).
Uncapped retrieval vs the derived cap If a retrieval-style mode ever lands (#6), note that an effectively-uncapped practice breaks the "cap = sum of budgets" derivation — the cap rule needs a story for it.
