Part 1 · The asks

Five asks, in the order to raise them

#1 Extension envelopes — one key in the schema the opener

The ask
Allow one property, extensions, on content and on each practice — namespaced keys (vector:*), under the rule an engine MUST load what it does not implement and MUST ignore it. Full writeup with the before/after: the extensions proposal.
Why
Our scenarios carry four fields v4 cannot say — answer_shape (right-answer vs open-reflection step) and three safety flags (elevated_stakes, threat_content, involves_minors). Today the only handoff option is deleting them. Measured on their own Marshall: flat fields → 4 scattered rejections; enveloped → rejections collapse to exactly one key kind. That one allowlisted key is the entire schema change.
The sentence that lands it
"Without the flags, a workplace-violence scenario loads in your engine with no crisis floor and no trace one ever existed." Not a style difference — a silent safety regression.
Expected pushback
"additionalProperties:false is deliberate — unknown fields are usually typos." Agree with the instinct: the envelope preserves it. A typo'd final_wrod still fails loudly; there is exactly one sanctioned place vendor data may live, and their §9.2 content lint applies inside it. On answer_shape specifically, expect "that's what levels.strong.look_for encodes" — true for a human reader, not machine-readable.
Fallback
Direct field adoption instead of the envelope (they add the four fields to the schema proper). Costs them a schema change per future field, but we win either way — our authoring keeps flat fields internally, and folding is only an export projection. If they say yes to either form: we hand them the schema diff and test fixtures same day.

#2 Stop making humans invent the mechanical fields

The ask
For fields their engine needs but no SME ever wrote — purpose on every phase and practice, the two transition.button_labels, final_word — agree they are engine defaults or generated at export, never hand-authored SME content.
Why — the decks never contained them
Searched the final 36-page WPV deck: purpose ×0, final_word ×0, misconception ×0, rubric ×0. And their own audit documents what filling required slots did:
"several locked final_word lines across the LOs are authored (spec-required) with no deck antecedent" · "likely filled to satisfy a schema slot, not SME-authored" · "Possible invented teaching point… no counterpart anywhere in the 15-slide deck. Compliance-relevant field."— their docs/authoring/sme-punch-list.md
Expected pushback
"The fields are cheap to write." The Marshall example answers it: cheap-to-write is how an invented compliance claim got into a shipped scenario. The issue isn't effort, it's provenance.
Fallback
We already ship house defaults on our side (measured from their own 11 scenarios), so nothing blocks us. The ask just makes the policy shared instead of ours alone.

#3 Retry — the decks asked for it; make it an option

The ask
A per-step "may retry" flag. Forward-only advancement stays the default everywhere the flag is absent — this adds an option, not a rewrite.
Why — it's their own note
"can trigger another scene progression to retry or play out further" · "the scenario continues until you see what works"— the Kendra deck (authored LXD intent)
"Recorded as a real deviation from authored deck intent; decide whether a bounded retry/mastery feature belongs on the roadmap."— their own sme-punch-list.md
They already logged dropping it as a deviation. We're seconding their own open item, with a shape (optional, per-step) that preserves their forward-only architecture.
Expected pushback
"Forward-only is a deliberate guarantee (derived caps, guaranteed close)." The per-step flag answer: a bounded retry re-enters the same practice with the same budget rules; the cap and the close survive.
Fallback
Roadmap acknowledgment. Neither player has retry today, so nothing regresses while it waits — but get it on the record as authored-intent, not our feature request.

#4 House conventions — two small decisions their content already needs

The ask (a)
Pick the practice-button convention. Measured across their 11 scenarios: the practice→debrief button is authored 29 times and 23 of them are the identical string "Talk it through" (exceptions include "Talk it out" — the same words rearranged). That's drift, not design; make it a default. The debrief→next button is the opposite — 17 of 29 distinct, story-carrying labels ("Sit down with Bianca", "Find Marco") — leave that one authored. Bonus finding: "Begin practicing" is used as both an opening and a debrief label. Their punch list already flags the sibling issue ("Last-debrief transition text splits 3-3 across the family… SME/LED to pick the house convention").
The ask (b)
Allow a partial quality scale on practices, the way the opening's levels already work. Most authored beats ground two tiers; v4 requires all three, and their own audit says the missing ones were "synthesized SME-faithfully" — i.e., invented. Either partial is legal, or every author invents a middle tier forever.
Expected pushback
On (b): "the classifier needs all three." Their own opening levels are partial by design, so the engine already handles it — the question is why practices are different.

#5 A clarifying question shouldn't cost a turn

The ask
Adopt the redirect rebate for coach steps: an off-script or clarifying learner turn re-asks without burning budget.
Why
Their spec is explicit the other way — for a coach practice, "a clarifying question is just a turn." On a 2-turn budget (their common case), one confused question burns half the learner's chance to actually practice. We hit exactly this and fixed it — the rebate ships in js/sim-core.js and every UX Universal scenario runs on it. Reference implementation available to diff against.
Expected pushback
"The cap is derived from budgets — rebates complicate it." Answer: the rebate is budget-inert by the same logic as their own coach-help turns (§7.2.4), which they already exempt from the cap.
Fallback
None needed — this is engine behavior on their side. If declined, learners on their player just have a worse time than on ours; worth stating that plainly and moving on.
Part 2 · The architecture session

#6 — Generalize [[spotted:]] into a pluggable interaction layer separate meeting

The ask
Their [[spotted:]] crediting contract — engine strips the marker, validates ids against the rubric, "the player's meter reads only this" — is exactly the right pattern: the engine consumes typed outputs and stays agnostic to the interaction. But it exists for observe_react only. Generalize it (credited-items independent of what the learner is looking at) and treat the interaction surface as pluggable, the way their format's oneOf already treats the interaction shape.
Why — their growth rule is half true
Their spec: "a new practice type is one new mode value plus one new interaction shape. The spine never changes." True of their schema; false of their engine — today every new mode is engine surgery. And the cost is already visible: teach-back (retrieval) is inexpressible in v4 precisely because the one output contract is welded to observe's required exhibit.
The proof to demo
UX Universal runs the same pattern generalized: the photo/hotspot canvas and the text-observation log are two surfaces over the same authored scenario and the same output contract, swapped by a flag with zero engine edits. Teach-back landed the same way. Three interactions, no runner surgery.
Why it's parked
It's an architecture discussion with real design work behind it, and bundling it with #1–#5 risks the small asks drowning. Raise it only if the room is going well, or book it separately.
Part 3 · Flag in passing

Small items — mention, don't debate

ItemWhat to say
help_turns default trap Their engine default (DEFAULT_HELP_TURNS = 2) silently turns the mid-scene help affordance ON for any scenario that doesn't author the field — their own audit had to write 0 everywhere because "no deck contemplates it." Suggest defaulting OFF.
Spec/schema disagreement on coach media The spec prose says a coach step's pinned media is a "reference image"; the schema allows video. Tiny, but it's the kind of drift their own alignment audit tracks — and the video reading matters to us (it's how watch-and-discuss maps).
Uncapped retrieval vs the derived cap If a retrieval-style mode ever lands (#6), note that an effectively-uncapped practice breaks the "cap = sum of budgets" derivation — the cap rule needs a story for it.
