      Designer notes

      • Tests: whether the learner can turn a messy set of reports into a clean document, separating what was seen from what was assumed, and leaving nothing important out. Evidence is their own prose, quoted back line by line.
      • Cost: one Haiku call per submit (max 400 output tokens, roughly 700 input tokens). Empty, very short or question-only submissions make no call. Each re-submit after editing is another call. Add ?offline=1 to force the stand-in checker.
      • Scripted vs live: the sheet, the five checklist points and the layout are scripted. The model decides which points are clearly covered and quotes lines to flag; code then drops any quote that is not an exact substring of the learner's text and keeps the checklist consistent with the surviving flags. The offline stand-in uses keyword patterns and a short word list, so it is cruder (it cannot judge invented detail beyond a small list).
      • Stand-in content: the scenario and the facts are written for this riff from general sharps practice. They are not official course copy.
      • A real build still needs: an authored fact set per scenario (with synonyms for each fact), review of how often the model misses or over-flags, a tone check, accessibility testing with a screen reader on the highlighted spans, and a decision on how many re-submits to allow.
