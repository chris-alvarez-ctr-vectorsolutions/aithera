The safe-sharps procedure

Four moves, in this order

1

Plan the disposal first

Have a plan for handling and disposal before you use a needle or other sharp instrument, or before you clean up broken glass.

2

Use a needle alternative, or a safer alternative, whenever one exists.

3

Activate the safety feature

If the device has a safety feature, activate it.

4

Straight into the container

Dispose of the used sharp directly into a designated sharps container, immediately or as soon as possible.

Chapter 7 · Why it works

Why the container beats the gloves

1

Heavy-duty gloves can help protect against cuts, punctures, and exposure, but even thick gloves can be penetrated by a needle.

A designated sharps container is an engineering control. It contains the sharp after disposal, reducing the chance that another person will come into contact with it. That provides stronger protection against needlestick injuries than relying on PPE alone.

Chapter 9 · What people actually do

The shortcut is the exception

There’s a common assumption that when a sharps container isn’t nearby, everyone takes the shortcut and drops the sharp in the nearest trash. Making the trip to a designated container can feel unrealistic when work is moving quickly.

Memorable isn’t the same as normal.

The shortcut stands out because it’s memorable and because of what can happen when someone takes it, not because it’s how the work normally gets done. The one time someone cuts a corner becomes the story people retell. The routine trips to the sharps container don’t. Safe disposal is the norm, even when the container isn’t convenient.

Source: Gershon et al. (2000), “Hospital Safety Climate and Its Relationship with Safe Work Practices and Workplace Exposure Incidents,” American Journal of Infection Control, 28(3), 211–221.
