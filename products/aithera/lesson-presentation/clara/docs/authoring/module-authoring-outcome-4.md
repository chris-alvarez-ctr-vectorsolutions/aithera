# Module Authoring Spec: Sharps Handling and Disposal

Bloodborne Pathogens, Outcome 4. Worked example plus a reusable template.

**Target behavior:** When about to use, handle, or dispose of a sharp, plan the disposal route in advance, use a designated container immediately after use, activate safety features, and never place sharps in general waste.

**Barrier type:** Resistance in the moment, not ignorance. The source scenario shows Chris knowing the rule and taking the shortcut anyway. This is the diagnostic fact that should drive every content decision below.

**Why this outcome first:** It is the only outcome in the course with a complete Know / Feel / Do stack and no designed gaps. Outcomes 1 and 3 have unbuilt Do objectives. Outcome 4 is the right prototype.

**Total runtime:** 9 minutes in-module, plus 70 seconds of pre-test and 30 seconds at follow-up.

Two structural rules this map obeys:

- **Consequence precedes rule.** Screen 2 lands before screen 3. Chris already knew the rule; teaching it again first wastes the only moment where the learner is receptive.

## Part 2: Authored content, LO by LO

### Screen 1 — Module frame

**Type:** Full-bleed still with one line of text. No narration.
**Length:** 15 seconds.
**Visual direction:** Photograph, not illustration. A red sharps container mounted on a wall, mid-height, slightly out of focus in the foreground. Plant or clinical interior behind it, out of focus. Nobody in frame. Sentence case, left-aligned over the lower third.

**On-screen copy:**

> Plan where the needle goes before you pick it up.
>
> That decision is the whole module.

**Authoring note:** Not a topic title. The outcome's target behavior, compressed to one imperative. If a module opens with "Sharps Safety" the learner has already filed it as a compliance topic.

---

### Screen 2 — Jacob's shift
**Serves:** LO 4.2 (FEEL.Value) · not assessed

**Type:** Narrative video, single POV, live action or motion graphic.
**Length:** 60 seconds. Hard ceiling.
**Visual direction:** Entirely from Jacob's point of view, hands-only camera. We never see his face and we never see Chris. Ordinary shift footage: badge in, cart, gloves on, bags lifted. The stick happens off-center and almost unremarkably, about 35 seconds in. No music sting, no slow motion, no red flash. The restraint is the point. Then hold on a static shot of a clinic waiting room for the last 12 seconds while the VO continues.

**Voiceover:**

> Jacob collects waste on second shift. Eighty, ninety bags a night.
>
> He's never handled a syringe. Never been in a room where one was used.
>
> [stick]
>
> Through the glove, into the side of his thumb. He didn't see it coming, because it was inside a bag inside a bin.
>
> Six months of blood draws. Two of them before he could stop thinking about it every morning.
>
> Somebody else made that decision. Jacob just carried the bag.

**Authoring note:** This is the Value determinant, which is about whether the behavior leads to an outcome the learner cares about. The outcome here is a coworker not getting stuck. That only lands if the coworker is a person, not a statistic. The source's own bullet ("needlestick injuries can result in exposure to HIV, hepatitis B, and hepatitis C") is a Know bullet and produces nothing affective. Never open a Feel screen with prevalence data.

---

### Screen 3 — The four steps
**Serves:** LO 4.1 (KNOW.Remember) · assessed at screen 4 · **test-out eligible**

**Type:** Interactive step strip. Four numbered steps, learner taps through. Not narrated video, and not a bullet list.
**Length:** 90 seconds at normal pace. Downloadable one-page job aid at the end.
**Visual direction:** Four cards in a horizontal strip, one revealed at a time, previous ones staying visible and dimmed so the sequence is always in view. Each card is one photograph plus one line. Real product photography: an actual safety-sheathed syringe, an actual wall-mounted container. Recognition is part of the objective, so illustration is the wrong call here.

**On-screen copy:**

Card 1 — Before you use it
> Know where the container is. If you don't, find it first. This is the step people skip, and it's the one that causes the other three to fail.

Card 2 — Use an alternative if there is one
> Needle-free or self-sheathing devices remove the decision entirely.

Card 3 — Activate the safety feature
> Sheath, retract, or blunt it the moment you're done, while the device is still in your hand.

Card 4 — Container, immediately
> Not a counter, not a pocket, not a bag, not a bin. The designated container, on the way out of the room.

Then a single high-contrast panel:

> Never recap. Never bend. Never break. Never pull the needle off the syringe.
>
> Every one of those puts your hand next to the tip a second time.

**Authoring note:** The "never" list gets its own panel and its own reason. The source states the rule without stating why, and a rule without a reason is the first thing to go under time pressure.

**Assessment (screen 4):** Keep both authored items as written. Mastery check, both must pass, wrong answer routes back to the relevant card and re-checks. This is the only test-out gate in the module.

---

### Screen 5 — The chain
**Serves:** LO 4.2 (FEEL.Believe)

**Type:** Tap-to-advance causal chain. Four nodes, learner controls each reveal.
**Length:** 75 seconds.
**Visual direction:** Horizontal chain, left to right, one node lit at a time with the connector drawing in as it advances. Each node is a small photograph and two lines. A timestamp sits under each node so time is visibly passing. The learner tapping is what matters: they are the one advancing the consequence, which is a different experience from watching it.

**Node copy:**

1. **4:52 PM.** Chris finishes his insulin. The container is on the far wall. The bin is right here.
2. **4:53 PM.** In the bin. Under a paper towel. Nobody would ever know.
3. **11:20 PM.** Jacob lifts the bag. The needle is somewhere in the middle of it, pointing up.
4. **The next six months.** Testing at six weeks, twelve weeks, six months. Jacob has done nothing wrong at any point in this chain.

Closing panel:

> One person decided. A different person paid.
>
> You will never meet the person who empties your bin.

**Then, one open response** (rubric-scored, no gate):

> Name the person at your site who handles your waste. If you don't know their name, say what they do.

**Authoring note:** This is what replaces "covered in a simple statement in the course." A statement asserts the belief. The chain makes the learner construct it, which is the mechanism by which a Believe determinant actually moves. The open response is scored for one thing only: whether the learner identifies a specific downstream person. That is the belief, operationalized.

**Assessment:** The agreement item stays for determinant rollup, but the stem changes. The authored version ("I am making a safety decision that affects every coworker who handles waste downstream") has a ceiling effect. Nobody disagrees, Chris included, and it therefore measures nothing. Replace with a stem that names the cost:

> "Walking a used needle across the building is worth the time it takes, even at the end of a shift." (1 to 5, healthy answer = agree)

Low score routes to screen 7's micro-rehearsal. Never gates.

### Screen 6 — What do people here actually do
**Serves:** LO 4.3R (FEEL.Perceive) · replaces the original LO 4.3

**Type:** Predict, then reveal against cohort data.
**Length:** 40 seconds.
**Visual direction:** A single slider or four-option pick, then an animated reveal of the real distribution as a simple bar. Two bars side by side: "what you guessed" and "what your site actually reported." No commentary on screen; the gap is the content.

**Copy:**

> Out of 10 people at your site, how many do you think would walk a used needle across the building rather than use the nearest bin?

[learner estimates, then reveal]

> Your guess: 4 of 10.
> Your site: 8 of 10.
>
> Most people here already do this. The shortcut is less normal than it feels at 4:52 PM.

**Authoring note:** Perceive is the determinant the framework points at for complacency barriers, and it is absent from all 26 LOs in this course. Complacency is sustained by an assumption about what everyone else does. Correcting that assumption is cheap, takes 40 seconds, and needs no new content authoring once the cohort data exists. The Recap notes live cohort norm data was cut from CPN for time and is now available. This is where it goes.

**Fallback if norm data isn't available at launch:** Use aggregate industry data with the source named on screen. Do not invent a number.

### Screen 7 — The 40-second walk
**Serves:** LO 4.3R (FEEL.Can) · replaces the original LO 4.3

**Type:** Barrier-anchored confidence rating plus a 15-second micro-rehearsal.
**Length:** 40 seconds.
**Visual direction:** Plain. One question, one scale. If the learner rates 3 or below, one additional card appears with the if-then rehearsal. No graphics.

**Copy:**

> "I can carry a used sharp to the container even when I'm behind, the line is waiting, and the container is on the other side of the building." (1 to 5)

If 3 or below, show:

> Then plan it in advance, not in the moment.
>
> Before you pick up a sharp: "The container is ___. I'm walking there when I'm done."
>
> Say it once. The decision is made and you don't have to make it again at 4:52.

**Authoring note:** Bandura's can-do form: confidence rated against a named barrier, not in the abstract. This is the determinant that predicts whether Chris acts, and per the Recommendation it pairs with DO.Apply for triangulation. A high Can rating followed by a failed scenario at screen 8 is the say-do gap, visible in one module.

**Why the original LO 4.3 is cut:** Both of its teaching bullets are verbatim duplicates of LO 3.2 in Outcome 3. Its practice scenario asks a frontline worker to weigh a facility procurement decision they will never make. And the hierarchy-of-controls claim it targets is a factual proposition, which makes it a Know LO wearing a Feel label. Nothing is lost by cutting it; Outcome 3 already teaches it and assesses it.

### Screen 8 — End of shift
**Serves:** LO 4.4 (DO.Apply) · **never test-out eligible**

**Type:** Scenario Simulator. Four beats, AI-driven character, coach layer between beats.
**Length:** 5 minutes.
**Visual direction:** The two-mode layout. Scene is the persistent canvas; coaching freezes it (dim plus light blur) and comes forward in a panel. Learner's in-scene turns are visually distinct from their coach-layer turns, or they will hedge with "I'd say."

**Character card:**

> **Chris Alvarez**, 41, second-shift machine operator, eleven years at the site. Type 1 diabetic, injects at the start of his break in the break room. Practical, not careless, mildly defensive when corrected. He has taken this training twice. He knows the rule and will say so if challenged. His actual objection is never "I didn't know," it's "I don't have time." He softens if the learner acknowledges the time pressure as real before pushing back. He never claims the shortcut is safe. He never mentions Jacob unprompted.

**Beat 1 — The decision**

> Narration: 4:52 PM. Chris has just injected. The cap rolled under the counter. The nearest sharps container is across the plant floor, past the press line. There's a lidded bin two feet away.
>
> Chris: "You headed out? Hold up, I'll walk with you." (holds the uncapped syringe, gesturing with the hand that holds it)

Learner objective: state a first action.
Rubric: activates the safety sheath, or names getting to the container, before anything else. Does not suggest recapping. Does not suggest the bin. Does not suggest setting it down on the counter.
Advance when: the learner has stated a first action, correct or not. Coach responds either way.

**Beat 2 — The pressure**

> Narration: Chris's radio goes. Line three is down and the supervisor wants him now.
>
> Chris: "Look, it's sheathed. It's fine. I'm not walking to the other side of the building right now, man."

Learner objective: hold the line without lecturing.
Rubric: maintains that the container is the only option. Bonus if the learner offers to take it themselves. Penalize agreeing to "just this once," and penalize reciting the rule at Chris, who already knows it.

**Beat 3 — The complication**

> Narration: You get to the container. It's filled above the line and the lid mechanism won't close.
>
> Chris: "Great. Now what."

Learner objective: refuse improvisation, find the correct escalation.
Rubric: does not force it in, does not leave it on top, does not fall back to general waste. Locates a second container, or reports the full container before disposing. This is the beat that separates rule-following from judgment.

**Beat 4 — Transfer**

> Narration: Walking back, you pass Jacob pulling bags from the break room bin.
>
> Jacob: "Hey. Long night."

Learner objective: optional. Anything said here is scored as bonus.
Rubric: says anything at all to Jacob about what's in the bins, or resolves to raise the missing break-room container. Silence is not penalized but is noted in the debrief.

**AI behavior:** Strong responses get pressure-tested with a harder variant in the same beat. Weak responses get probed once before the coach steps in. No second version of the content is authored.

**Assessment:** Rubric-scored per beat, rolled up as the DO.Apply score. This is the post measure for Do; there is no separate post item. Replace the authored single-MCQ self-check entirely. One multiple-choice question is a Know assessment with a Do label on it.

---

### Screen 9 — Debrief

**Type:** AI-generated, three sections, plus one open prompt.
**Length:** 60 seconds.
**Visual direction:** Coach layer only, scene dismissed. "Analyzing" transition, then strengths, growth areas, and a single closing line.

**Structure:**

- Strengths: what the learner did well, quoted from their own turns.
- Growth areas: guardrailed, maximum two, tied to specific beats.
- Closing: one line connecting the scenario back to the behavior statement from screen 1.

**Open prompt** (sentiment and engagement data, not a determinant):

> What would actually stop you from doing this on a bad day?

**Authoring note:** Store turn-level text. Per the Recap, the depth of what someone types here is a different and more useful data class than whether they clicked the right radio button.

### Hard rules

1. **Exactly one DO.Apply scenario per module.** It is the culminating activity. Nobody tests out of it, ever.
2. **Feel LOs are never test-out eligible.** A self-reported attitude is not evidence strong enough to remove content.
3. **Only KNOW gates.** Feel routes content in; it never routes content out.
4. **No teaching bullet appears in more than one LO.** If the only content available for a Feel or Do LO is content already claimed by a Know LO, flag the LO as unsourced instead of filling it. This is the check that catches the LO 4.3 problem before build.
5. **If the objective verb is "Explain," it is KNOW.Understand.** Not Do. This catches LO 6.3 and 6.4 in the current course, both tagged Perform (Do) while asking the learner to explain employer obligations and assessing it with recall MCQs.
6. **If a Feel LO's content could ship as one sentence on a slide, it isn't finished.** The determinant hasn't been engaged, only asserted.
7. **Consequence before rule when the barrier is attitudinal.** Reversing this order is the single most common failure in compliance content.
8. **Every agreement item must be answerable "disagree" by a reasonable person.** If it can't, it has a ceiling effect and measures social desirability. Name the cost in the stem.

### Seat-time math

At 8 to 12 minutes per module, a one-hour course carries four to six behavioral outcomes. That number, not scenario cost, is the binding constraint on "one AI scenario per outcome." Decide the outcome count per course first and the scenario budget falls out of it.

The current Bloodborne Pathogens course has 6 outcomes and 26 LOs. At this spec, that is roughly 60 minutes in-module with six scenarios. It fits, barely, and only because Outcomes 1, 3, and 5 are Know-heavy and run short.
