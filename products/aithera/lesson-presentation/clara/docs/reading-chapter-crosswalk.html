The rule this map follows

The chapter ID is the one key shared by all three formats. The module’s existing rules turn the pre-check into a list of chapter IDs. Video plays those clips, the podcast plays those files, and the article shows those sections. The article never decides routing itself. It shows what it’s given and reports each answer under the same key the video screens use, so the results screen stays unchanged.

A section is in the article only if it is in the library. Chapter slots use the deck’s own wording. V2’s scenes stay as the frame around them.

For review before the build

The opening becomes deck wording

V2’s opening tells 04A in its own words (“Jacob is collecting workplace trash”). In the slot it becomes the deck’s sentences (“Jacob collects the bag for sanitation”), in V2’s order.

Recommend: accept, so the three formats say the same thing.

V2’s procedure scene goes

Chris’s insulin syringe, the “return to Chris’s workspace” cards and the safety-sheath before-and-after belong to a K1 story with no remedial or test-up version.

Recommend: drop the cards, keep the sheath before-and-after as an illustration of “activate any available safety feature.”

Wrapping conflicts with 01R

Manufacturing 01R says you may need to wrap a blade’s exposed edge, per facility procedure. V2’s takeaway says not to substitute soft wrapping for disposal. The Law Enforcement 01R agrees with V2.

Recommend: limit the takeaway line to needles, or match the deck’s “follow your facility’s procedure.”

The evidence and F3 pull in opposite directions

The evidence section shows 34 of 40 injuries came from needles in soft-waste sacks. F3 later says the shortcut is the exception (92.7% always use the container). The figures don’t conflict, but the first can be read as “everyone does it.”

Recommend: one bridging line in the evidence section saying these are injury records, not how often the shortcut happens.

Situation 2 repeats K2

“A container is full” covers the same ground as the K2 check a few sections earlier.

Recommend: keep it as practice (unscored), or swap it for a scenario the checks don’t cover.
