Vector Solutions · Knowledge Layer · Working concept

The unit of our product is a behavior, not a course.

Decompose a course into behavioral outcomes. Populate each one across Know, Feel and Do. Have a SME sign the objectives and their metadata — once. Everything a learner ever sees is derived from that signed substrate, which is why we can stand behind it in any permutation, in any context, in any modality.

The problem with "AI content"

Anyone can generate a course. Generation is not the differentiator, and pricing a generated course against a generated course is a race we do not want to run.

What we sell instead

A validated behavioral substrate and a development process built on it. The output is not "content about harassment" — it is a set of behaviors a workforce can be shown to perform.

Why the framework is the moat

Know·Feel·Do is a chain of conditions, not a taxonomy. A course that only covers Know is not a shorter version of behavior change; it is a different product that doesn't produce it.

01

Where quality is created, and where it is signed

Two signatures, in two different places, doing two different jobs. The SME signs the substrate — the Know·Feel·Do mapping of objectives to behavioral outcomes and the metadata on each objective. The LED signs the render — the specific recomposed experience chosen from what the Learning Layer proposes. Everything between the two signatures is derivation, which is the only reason this scales.

04

What the Learning Layer hands the LED

Not a finished course, and not a blank page. Three or four candidate module structures, each scored on how well it holds up under the four capabilities — because a structure that is beautiful at full length and collapses when three objectives are compressed out is not a structure we can sell. The LED picks the starting point and signs the result.

05

Provenance today, evidence tomorrow

We will not have outcome data on day one, and the story cannot depend on it. It can, however, be built so that the data accrues automatically — and so that the system improves itself from what it collects.

Phase 1 · At launch

Provenance

Every objective a learner encounters traces to a SME-signed KFD mapping with its theoretical construct attached. The claim is about process, and it is verifiable: we can show the chain from the behavior to the objective to the beat on screen.

"Every minute of this is there because a named condition of behavior change requires it."
Phase 2 · Accruing

Performance

Each module closes on a simulation that produces a graded performance record against a SME-validated rubric, not a completion tick. Objective-level data accumulates across the cohort rather than being extracted from every individual.

"We can tell you which behaviors your workforce can actually perform, by objective."
Phase 3 · Compounding

Evidence — and self-correction

With enough records, the derivation rules become testable. If the manufacturing-lens podcast cut of a module underperforms the reference video on the same rubric, the Learning Layer down-ranks it and tells the LEDs. Permutations that work get promoted; permutations that don't get reworked.

"The system gets better at recomposition because it watches what recomposition produces."
06

Decisions this model forces

Each of these is a genuine fork, not a detail. The concept holds either way — but the product, the roadmap and the sales story differ materially depending on the answer.

D1

Are alternative modalities clean, or fully wrapped?

A podcast wrapped in a gating pre-check and a culminating simulation is no longer the thing that made podcasts appealing. A clean standalone podcast cannot produce a mastery record or satisfy an attestation. The likely answer is that this is a customer setting rather than a product decision — but it is a setting that changes what the modality is for.

clean standalone (engagement only) · fully inclusive (record-bearing) · admin-selectable per course
D2

Do break points ship in v1?

Break points — the article that stops to ask a pathing question, the podcast chapter boundary that hands off to the LMS — are what stop passive modalities from degrading mastery-based sequencing. Without them, non-branching modalities can only serve pre-rendered cuts. They may be the single highest-leverage small feature in the model.

v1 · fast-follow · permanent limitation (pre-rendered cuts only)
D3

What is the sector default for test-out?

Compression is the mechanism; how much of it a customer gets is a setting. Education, Public and Commercial will not want the same defaults, and compliance-locked objectives need to test up rather than disappear so that seat time is preserved and better spent. One library, different dials.

test-out on/off · compliance lock per objective · seat-time floor enforced
D4

Can the pre-experience ever exempt a whole module?

Under attested coverage the answer is no — the learner always enters the module and always performs the culminating simulation, which doubles as the verification that they needed none of the instruction. That is a defensible position, but it caps how short a compressed experience can get, and it makes simulation throughput a scaling constraint rather than a feature.

simulation is always the floor · module-level exemption for non-regulated content only
