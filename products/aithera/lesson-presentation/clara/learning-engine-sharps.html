01

What the engine is, and is not

Three common assumptions to rule out first.

Not a content generator

Content is authored and signed off in the Knowledge Layer. The engine only selects among existing permutations.

Upstream: the Knowledge Layer

Not a learning management system

Assignments, rosters, and transcripts remain the LMS's. It launches a composite and receives back "in progress" and "complete" — nothing more.

Downstream: the LMS

Not AI-powered (at least not directly)

The engine is deterministic: every decision follows fixed rules applied to content and learner evidence. AI lives upstream, in Knowledge Layer content creation, and in the AI-roleplay modality the engine may select.

Rules, not a model

What it is

A sequencer and a judge: it orders content, selects each item's version, asks the questions that prove mastery, and records proof at the finest useful grain — the individual learning objective.

Why that matters

Deterministic rules plus per-objective evidence mean identical treatment for identical evidence, credit that follows a learner across training, and reporting precise to what was proven and when.

03

A learner's path through a module

The module contract: every module, in every modality, follows the same phases — which is what lets one be shortened, re-dressed for a sector, moved between composites, or turned into a podcast without breaking. Click a phase.

PHASE 1
Pre battery
A short set of Know and Feel questions before any instruction. Never skipped.
PHASE 2
Learn and check
Each learning object in order, then its check right after it. A failed check does not stop anyone.
PHASE 3
Remediation
Anything missed comes back in a different modality, followed by a fresh question.
PHASE 4 · OPTIONAL
Post battery
Holds checks a modality could not ask inline (a podcast), plus any "after" item not re-asked during the module. Not a test.
TERMINAL
Mastered
Every object at its terminal state. The next module unlocks.
Missed Know and Feel objects come back before the Do objects; missed Do objects come back after the other Do objects, before mastery.
Final exam

None. Mastery is proven as the learner goes, not tested at the end. Modules are strictly sequential; the composite completes when every module is mastered.

04

What counts as mastery

The rule in one sentence

A learner masters a module by satisfying every gating object: passing the knowledge check for Know objects, meeting the rubric for Do objects. Nothing is tested twice, nothing at the end.

This replaces the post-test. Evidence is per objective — what this learner proved, and when — not a pass/fail exam over the whole composite. That grain enables the rest of the model.

  • Credit follows a learner across composites: we know exactly which objectives, and therefore which modules, they proved.
  • Reporting runs at the objective level — the grain of the evidence.
  • A learner who fails one thing repeats one thing, not a whole exam.

Not every object gates; each carries an author-chosen assessment policy. Click one to see how a wrong answer plays out.

None
No question is ever asked.
Typical use: introductions, summaries, connective material.
Ask
Asked of a sample of learners; the answer is recorded and never blocks. Sampling spreads the load — each learner sees a few, the cohort covers every item.
Typical use: insight and measurement items: climate, prevalence, norms.
Remediate
A question is asked; an unfavorable answer earns one retry in a different modality, then the learner moves on.
Typical use: attitudes we want to shift, and credit-bearing items that should not block.
holds the module open
Gate
A question is asked and must be passed. The learner keeps remediating until it is. No attempt limit.
Typical use: the load-bearing objectives. Only these hold a module open.

Feel objectives are never gates

An attitude has a desired direction but cannot be forced, so it cannot gate — a Feel item is Ask or Remediate. The "after" is always taken, even after a favorable "before": it is a measurement, not proof, and can move either direction.

Do objectives normally are gates

The behavior is the point of the module, so the performance is what holds it open until it is met.

06

Questions: what is asked, of whom, and why

Every question does one or more of four jobs. Asking all of them of all learners would double seat time, so the engine is deliberate about who sees what.

Prove

Prove mastery of an objective, and through it the module.

Route

Send the learner the right way: test-out, test-up, remediation.

Measure efficacy

The before-and-after on knowledge gained and attitude shifted.

Gather insight

Climate, prevalence, norms: what the organization looks like from the inside.

ItemWho sees it, and when
Gating and remediating itemsAsked in the pre battery and again as the check after the object — the load-bearing items.
Know items that could test a learner out or upAsked in the pre battery regardless of policy: skipping or leveling up the content justifies the question.
Feel insight itemsSampled: each learner sees a subset, the cohort covers every item, and the same subset serves as that learner's before and after.
Question banks, not fixed questionsEach objective has a bank; learners draw items they haven't seen. Leaked items are swapped or retired.

Sampling across a cohort

Six learners, two Feel insight items: each answers one, together covering both three times over. No one sits through every measurement question; Know items that could test someone out are still asked of all.

Item A  Item B

A fresh question every time

A missed pre-battery question draws a different one from the same bank at the post-object check, then another at remediation — a learner never re-answers a Know question they've seen. Feel is the exception: the same statement is re-asked so before and after can be compared.

07

Assessing behavior

Do objectives are assessed by performance. The rubric is fixed; only the permutation differs, each with its own key mapping performance to the rubric's criteria.

Do · D1 One rubric per objective

A short list of what a passing performance must show, written about the behavior, not about any one scenario.

  • Chose a tactic suited to the moment and its constraints
  • Held the position when the other person pushed back
  • Followed up afterward with the person affected

Placeholder criteria. Applied unchanged to every permutation below.

The definite Do permutation is AI roleplay: the learner rehearses live against an AI character who pushes back, with in-the-moment coaching. A model call scores the transcript against the rubric at session end. It is the preferred experience and can reinforce the module's other objectives.

Cheaper equivalents are under consideration for cost control. Only modalities scoreable against the same rubric qualify — a passive video gives nothing to score.

A failed performance is remediated like anything else: a different permutation or a different scenario, then scored again, with no attempt limit.

09

Credit that follows the learner

Everything a learner proves is recorded once, against the learner, at the objective level, and never overwritten. Whether it counts toward a given composite depends on the goal's recency window — how old proof may be before it must be re-earned. Drag the window.

365 days (the default)Settable per customer and per launch.

Mastered within the window

Skipped entirely, pre battery included, in any other composite that contains it — "New Hire Safety Orientation" does not make anyone redo Contain the Sharp.

Objectives proven within the window

Even inside a module that was never finished, they are skipped inside that module next time.

A restart is a new goal

With a fresh window. What carries in is whatever is still recent enough.

Older than the window

Not deleted. It simply stops counting until it is re-proven.

There is no "failed"

No attempt limit, no failing end state — a composite stays in progress until complete, matching the current JAM course paradigm.

Restart means a new goal

The LMS won't accept further updates once it records completion. Starting over creates a new goal; the recency window decides what carries in.

Review is always available for what has been mastered

Any mastered module, and the whole composite after completion, reopens in any modality with no questions and no effect on the record. Nothing unmastered is reviewable.

The LMS tells us very little about the learner

Content Hub passes who, which composite, and language — not sector, role, or location. Sector is looked up from the customer; role needs LMS changes.

The LMS never controls the experience

It supplies identifiers and launch settings and receives progress. Everything in between is the engine's.

12

Scope

Two efforts, not to be confused: the production first cut, and a customer-facing prototype gathering input before the roadmap locks.

Production first cut (v1) · in

  • Composite, module, object structure; modules in strict order
  • Pre battery on every module; test-out and test-up on Know (remediation on Feel)
  • Knowledge checks as the mastery gate; no final exam
  • Remediation until passed, in a fresh permutation
  • Do objectives scored by rubric; AI roleplay plus a cheaper equivalent, chosen by cost control
  • Learner-chosen permutation; sector and role contextualization
  • Cohort sampling of insight items
  • Credit across composites with a recency window
  • Content Hub launch with every Content Hub launch setting plus the engine's own; SCORM dispatch
  • Review of mastered content

Deferred

  • Assignment-level LMS integration (goals as real assignments)
  • Continuous goals (keep mastery current over time)
  • Jurisdiction-based contextualization
  • Engagement-based modality escalation
  • Feed-based learning (intermixing modules and/or learning objects)
  • Mobile app and embedded modes
  • Branching scenarios, simulations, flashcards, drills, mixed media as modalities
  • Learner intervention when someone is stuck; gamification; regulatory watch; effectiveness monitoring

The customer-facing prototype

13

Open items

Post battery contents

Whether it carries only deferred checks and matched "after" items, or also end-of-module sentiment and feedback.

Once-per-learner questions

Some questions (climate, prevalence, certain Feel items) belong once per learner within a window, not once per module or composite — asked in the learner's first composite, then not again until the window passes. The engine needs a home for questions with no learning object and a record of when each was last answered.

Glossary

Battery (pre, post)A set of questions asked together. The pre battery opens every module; the optional post battery closes one.
Behavioral outcomeThe one observable action a module exists to produce.
Break pointThe moment between two learning objects where a knowledge check is asked.
CardOne thing the engine hands the Experience Layer to show: a battery, a learning object with its check, or a system message.
CompositeAn ordered list of modules; what a customer assigns and what an LMS sees as a course.
Cost controlThe Vector-side rule that decides whether a learner gets the AI permutation of a Do object or a cheaper one.
GateAn assessment policy meaning the check must be passed. Only gating objects hold a module open.
GoalThe engine's record of one learner on one composite under one set of launch settings — today an attempt, later an assignment.
Know, Feel, DoVector's behavior-change framework: what a learner must understand, feel, and be able to do for a behavior to change. Every learning object's objective matches one of its subscales.
Knowledge checkThe question (or, for Do, the performance) asked right after a learning object.
LockedAlways delivered: required by law or policy, retained by the admin, or pivotal enough to reinforce regardless. Never skipped; served harder if already proven.
ModalityThe form a permutation takes: video, audio, text, AI tutor, AI roleplay.
PermutationOne rendering of a learning object in one modality. All permutations of an object teach the same thing and are assessed the same way.
Recency windowHow old proof may be and still count for a goal. Default one year.
RemediationRe-delivering something the learner failed or responded undesirably to (for Feel objects), in a permutation they have not seen, followed by a fresh question.
RubricThe list of things a passing performance must show for a Do objective. One per objective, applied to every permutation.
Test-out / Test-upSkipping unlocked content the learner has proven; serving locked content the learner has proven in a more demanding permutation.
