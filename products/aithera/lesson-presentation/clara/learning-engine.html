Learning Layer · the engine

The part that decides what you see next

Moment by moment, the Learning Engine reads how a piece of training is built and everything a learner has already proven, then hands the screen a short list of things to show: a few questions, a learning object with its check, a repair for something missed. It never draws a screen itself.

01

What the engine is, and is not

The fastest way to understand the engine is to rule out three things people assume it must be.

Not a content generator

Content is written and signed off in the Knowledge Layer before the engine ever sees it. The engine only chooses among permutations (finished versions) that already exist.

Upstream: the Knowledge Layer

Not a learning management system

Assignments, rosters and transcripts stay where they are. The LMS launches a composite and gets back "in progress" and "complete." That is the whole relationship.

Downstream: the LMS

Not AI-powered (at least not directly)

The engine is deterministic. Every decision follows from a fixed set of rules, given the content and the learner's evidence. The AI lives upstream in the Knowledge Layer, in content creation and management, and in the AI roleplay modality the engine can choose to serve.

Rules, not a model

What it is

A sequencer and a judge. It decides the order things are shown in, decides which version of each thing to show, asks the questions that prove mastery, and records what each learner has proven at the finest useful grain: the individual learning objective.

Why that matters

Because the engine is deterministic and the evidence is per objective, the same learner gets the same treatment for the same evidence, credit can follow a person across different training, and reporting can say exactly which things a person proved and when.

03

A learner's path through a module

The module contract: every module, in every modality, follows the same phases. That consistency is what lets a module be shortened for someone who already knows the material, re-dressed for a different sector, moved into another composite, or turned into a podcast without falling apart. Click a phase.

PHASE 1
Pre battery
A short set of Know and Feel questions before any instruction. Never skipped.
PHASE 2
Learn and check
Each learning object in order, then its check right after it. A failed check does not stop anyone.
PHASE 3
Remediation
Anything missed comes back in a different modality, followed by a fresh question.
PHASE 4 · OPTIONAL
Post battery
Holds checks a modality could not ask inline (a podcast), plus any "after" item not re-asked during the module. Not a test.
TERMINAL
Mastered
Every object at its terminal state. The next module unlocks.
Missed Know and Feel objects come back before the Do objects; missed Do objects come back after the other Do objects, before mastery.
Final exam

There is none. The end-of-course assessment is replaced by an "assess and prove mastery as we go" paradigm. Modules are strictly sequential: when every module is mastered, the composite is complete.

04

What counts as mastery

The rule in one sentence

A learner masters a module by satisfying each of its gating learning objects as they go: passing the knowledge check for Know objects and meeting the rubric for Do objects. Nothing is tested twice, and nothing is tested at the end.

This replaces the traditional post-test. Instead of a pass/fail exam over the whole composite, the evidence is per objective: which specific things this learner has proven, and when. That grain is what makes the rest of the model possible.

  • Credit can follow a learner across composites, because we know exactly which objectives, and therefore which modules, they proved.
  • Reporting can be at the objective level, because that is the grain of the evidence.
  • A learner who fails one thing repeats one thing, not a whole exam.

Not every learning object gates. Each carries an assessment policy chosen by the author. Click one to see how a wrong answer plays out.

None
No question is ever asked.
Typical use: introductions, summaries, connective material.
Ask
A question is asked of a sample of learners and the answer recorded. It never blocks the learner. Sampling spreads the burden: each learner sees a few of these; across the cohort every item is covered.
Typical use: insight and measurement items: climate, prevalence, norms.
Remediate
A question is asked; an unfavorable answer earns one retry in a different modality, then the learner moves on.
Typical use: attitudes we want to shift, and credit-bearing items that should not block.
holds the module open
Gate
A question is asked and must be passed. The learner keeps remediating until it is. No attempt limit.
Typical use: the load-bearing objectives. Only these hold a module open.

Feel objectives are never gates

An attitude statement has a desirable direction, but an attitude cannot be forced, so it cannot be a gate. In practice a Feel item is Ask or Remediate. And a Feel "after" is always taken, even when the "before" was already favorable: it is a measurement, not a proof, and an attitude can move the wrong way as easily as the right one.

Do objectives normally are gates

The behavior is the point of the module, so the performance is what holds it open until it is met.

06

Questions: what is asked, of whom, and why

Every question does one or more of four jobs. Asking every question of every learner would double seat time, so the engine is deliberate about who sees what.

Prove

Prove mastery of an objective, and through it the module.

Route

Send the learner the right way: test-out, test-up, remediation.

Measure efficacy

The before-and-after on knowledge gained and attitude shifted.

Gather insight

Climate, prevalence, norms: what the organization looks like from the inside.

ItemWho sees it, and when
Gating and remediating itemsAlways asked in the pre battery and again as the check after the object. These are the load-bearing items.
Know items that could test a learner out or upAlways asked in the pre battery, whatever their policy. Skipping or leveling up the content is worth the question.
Feel insight itemsSampled. Each learner sees a subset; across the cohort every item gets covered. The same subset is used for that learner's before and after, so the shift is measurable.
Question banks, not fixed questionsEach objective has a bank; learners see items they have not seen. If items leak, items are added or swapped.

Sampling across a cohort

Six learners, two Feel insight items. Each learner answers one; together they cover both, three times over. Nobody sits through every measurement question, and Know items that could test someone out are still asked of everyone.

Item A  Item B

A fresh question every time

Miss a question in the pre battery and the check after the lesson draws a different question from the same bank. Fail that and remediation draws another. The learner never re-answers a Know question they have seen. Feel is the deliberate exception: the same agreement statement is asked again so the before and after can be compared.

07

Assessing behavior

Do objectives are assessed by performance. The rubric is the same however the learner performs; what differs is the permutation, and each permutation carries its own key mapping what the learner did to the rubric's criteria.

Do · D1 One rubric per objective

A short list of what a passing performance must show, written about the behavior, not about any one scenario.

  • Chose a tactic suited to the moment and its constraints
  • Held the position when the other person pushed back
  • Followed up afterward with the person affected

Placeholder criteria. Applied unchanged to every permutation below.

The definite Do permutation is AI roleplay, the scenario simulator. The learner rehearses the behavior live with an AI character who pushes back, with coaching in the moment. The transcript is scored against the rubric by a model call at the end of the session. This is the preferred experience and the reference rendering; with AI coaching it can reinforce the module's other objectives as well.

Cheaper equivalents are under consideration for when cost must be controlled. Only modalities whose responses can be scored against the same rubric qualify. A passive video gives nothing to score.

A failed performance is remediated like anything else: a different permutation or a different scenario, then scored again, with no attempt limit.

Audience is handled in two places, on purpose

When two audiences need different instruction because their targeted behaviors differ, they get different composites built from different modules. 09

Credit that follows the learner

Everything a learner proves is recorded once, against the learner, at the objective level. Nothing is ever overwritten. Whether a piece of evidence counts in a given composite depends on the goal's recency window: how old proof may be before it has to be earned again. Drag the window.

365 days (the default)Settable per customer and per launch.
counts toward this goal
Today: new launch

Mastered within the window

The module is skipped entirely in any other composite that contains it, pre battery included. The Speak Up series does not make anyone redo Step In.

Objectives proven within the window

Even inside a module that was never finished, they are skipped inside that module next time.

A restart is a new goal

With a fresh window. What carries in is whatever is still recent enough.

Older than the window

Not deleted. It simply stops counting until it is re-proven.

How it fits with the LMS

In the first cut, composites launch from Content Hub like any other learning activity. From the LMS's point of view nothing changes: it launches what it sees as a course, and it hears back "in progress" and then "complete."

There is no "failed"

No attempt limit and no failing end state, so a composite is in progress until it is complete. This aligns with the current JAM course paradigm.

Restart means a new goal

Once the LMS records a completion it will not accept further updates on that attempt. Starting over creates a new goal, and the recency window decides what carries in.

Review is always available for what has been mastered

A learner can reopen any mastered module, and the whole composite after completion, in any modality, with no questions and no effect on their record. Nothing unmastered is reviewable.

The LMS tells us very little about the learner

Content Hub passes who and which composite, plus language. Not sector, role or location. Sector is looked up from what we know about the customer. Role will need LMS changes.

The LMS never controls the experience

It supplies identifiers and launch settings and receives progress. Everything in between is the engine's.

Scope

Two efforts are under way and should not be confused: the production first cut, and a customer-facing prototype built to gather input before the roadmap is locked.

Production first cut (v1) · in

  • Composite, module, object structure; modules in strict order
  • Pre battery on every module; test-out and test-up on Know (remediation on Feel)
  • Knowledge checks as the mastery gate; no final exam
  • Remediation until passed, in a fresh permutation
  • Do objectives scored by rubric; AI roleplay plus a cheaper equivalent, chosen by cost control
  • Learner-chosen permutation; sector and role contextualization
  • Cohort sampling of insight items
  • Credit across composites with a recency window
  • Content Hub launch with every Content Hub launch setting plus the engine's own; SCORM dispatch
  • Review of mastered content

Deferred

  • Assignment-level LMS integration (goals as real assignments)
  • Continuous goals (keep mastery current over time)
  • Jurisdiction-based contextualization
  • Engagement-based modality escalation
  • Feed-based learning (intermixing modules and/or learning objects)
  • Mobile app and embedded modes
  • Branching scenarios, simulations, flashcards, drills, mixed media as modalities
  • Learner intervention when someone is stuck; gamification; regulatory watch; effectiveness monitoring

The customer-facing prototype

A demonstration built to show the market what an ideal recomposed composite looks like and to gather input before the production roadmap is locked. It is not the v1 engine and does not need to be production quality. What it must do:

One compositeA single topic, decomposed into modules and reassembled end to end.
Two defined audiencesTwo sectors, two roles. Not every permutation.
Two or three modalitiesEnough to show choice without full production.
Test-out and test-upWith continuity under removal: the module still reads well when a piece is taken out.
ScoreableA scorecard we and customers can rate the prototype against.

13

Open items

Post battery contents

Whether the post battery carries only deferred checks and matched "after" items, or also end-of-module sentiment and feedback questions.

Once-per-learner questions

Some questions (climate, prevalence, certain Feel items) should be answered once per learner within a window, not once per module or per composite: asked in the first composite the learner takes, then not again until the window passes and they train again. The engine needs a home for questions that belong to no learning object and a way to know when a learner last answered them.

Glossary

Battery (pre, post)A set of questions asked together. The pre battery opens every module; the optional post battery closes one.
Behavioral outcomeThe one observable action a module exists to produce.
Break pointThe moment between two learning objects where a knowledge check is asked.
CardOne thing the engine hands the Experience Layer to show: a battery, a learning object with its check, or a system message.
CompositeAn ordered list of modules; what a customer assigns and what an LMS sees as a course.
Cost controlThe Vector-side rule that decides whether a learner gets the AI permutation of a Do object or a cheaper one.
GateAn assessment policy meaning the check must be passed. Only gating objects hold a module open.
GoalThe engine's record of one learner working on one composite under one set of launch settings. Today it plays the role of an attempt; later it will represent an assignment.
Know, Feel, DoVector's behavior-change framework: what a learner must understand, feel, and be able to do for a behavior to change. Every learning object's objective matches one of its subscales.
Knowledge checkThe question (or, for Do, the performance) asked right after a learning object.
LockedA learning object that is always delivered: a law or policy requires it, the admin does not want it removed, or it is pivotal enough to reinforce even when already known. Never skipped; served harder if already proven.
ModalityThe form a permutation takes: video, audio, text, AI tutor, AI roleplay.
PermutationOne rendering of a learning object in one modality. All permutations of an object teach the same thing and are assessed the same way.
Recency windowHow old proof may be and still count for a goal. Default one year.
RemediationRe-delivering something the learner failed or responded undesirably to (for Feel objects), in a permutation they have not seen, followed by a fresh question.
RubricThe list of things a passing performance must show for a Do objective. One per objective, applied to every permutation.
Test-out / Test-upSkipping unlocked content the learner has proven; serving locked content the learner has proven in a more demanding permutation.
