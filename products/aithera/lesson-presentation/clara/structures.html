Candidate structures

Not a finished course and not a blank page. Three structures the same 14 objectives can be poured into, each scored on how well it holds across the four capabilities — because a structure that is elegant at full length and collapses when three objectives drop out is not one we can sell.

Option A

Continuous narrative

One storyline for the length of the module: a single situation from first signal to resolution, with beats attached to points in the story.

  • Mastery-based sequencing
  • Contextualization
  • Assembly
  • Transformation

Strongest engagement, weakest under compression — remove three objectives and the story loses its connective tissue.

Proposed · LED signed Option B

Case ladder

Three escalating cases, each self-contained, each raising the judgment required. Objectives map cleanly to cases, so a case can be added, hardened or dropped whole.

  • Mastery-based sequencing
  • Contextualization
  • Assembly
  • Transformation

The workhorse. Compression and assembly operate on whole cases, so cohesion is structural rather than editorial. Default for regulated content — and what the shipped experience runs.

Option C

Coach-led rehearsal

An AI coach frames the behavior, the learner practices it immediately, and instruction arrives as debrief between reps rather than up front.

  • Mastery-based sequencing
  • Contextualization
  • Assembly
  • Transformation

Most differentiated, least portable — it barely survives translation to podcast or article. Reserve for high-stakes Do-heavy outcomes.

Why this learner's path looks like this

The chain behind the shipped permutation, end to end. Nothing in it was decided case by case — every step is computed from the objective metadata the SME signed. This is the claim that holds on day one, before there is any outcome data at all.

  1. The signed objectives. Module BO-2 “Step In” · K3 Know / Remember (recall the five Ds) and K4 Know / Evaluate (select the tactic best suited to a situation and constraint). Theoretical construct: Response Knowledge. Signed once, at the spec. SME signature
  2. The evidence. The entry check asked one Know item mapped to K4 — a tactic-selection question, not a definition question, because what a correct answer buys is the tactics instruction. Answered correctly; K3 is entailed by K4 at this level and the two are declared a cluster in the envelope.
  3. The derivation. Test-out suppressed the two instructional beats carrying K3 and K4 — The Five Ds and Pick your move — and shortened the intro clip, whose “how to respond” half the same item evidences. 2 of 14 objectives verified, 12 instructed, ~3 minutes removed.
  4. What could not move. The five Feel objectives and the four Do objectives were untouched: no entry item probes them, and the envelope marks belief and performance objectives as non-suppressible by design. The compliance-locked mastery item was served, not removed.
  5. The floor. The Scenario Simulator ran regardless — a learner who tested out of every instructional objective still performs the behavior against the SME-validated rubric. LED signature

Prototype. Capability scores and permutation verdicts are representative — the point is that they are computed and surfaced before a build, not that these particular numbers are final.

The battery plan

Five items, asked once, before any instruction — and what each answer is allowed to change. Never a Do objective: a question cannot credibly measure behavior, so D1/D2/D3 are only ever evidenced in the scenario or the follow-up.

ItemObjectiveCorrect answer changesMissed answer changes
1 · Ordering taskK1 (gate) Procedure, Case 1 and the in-flow check all drop from the path. Nothing — taught in full, then checked, as if the item were never asked.
2 · Misconception itemK2 (remediate, locked) The explainer serves its harder permutation; its check is skipped, not deferred. Nothing — explainer and check run exactly as they would with no battery at all.
3 · Recognition itemK3 (gate, locked) Both cases serve their harder, three-way-arguable variant — content-locked, so still shown, just not re-scored. Nothing — both cases run at their base difficulty, fully graded.
4 · Belief statementF1 (remediate) Sets a baseline only. Re-asked identically after the account (case 4); the reportable figure is the movement between the two, not either alone.
5 · Norm-perception statementF3 (remediate) Sets a baseline only. Re-asked identically at the cohort debrief; same movement-not-level logic as F1.
