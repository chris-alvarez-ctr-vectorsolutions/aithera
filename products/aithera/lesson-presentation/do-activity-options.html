“Do” activity options

Seven ways for a learner to show they've taken in a module, priced between "no model calls at all" and the full scenario simulator. Each one asks the learner to do something and records what they did. Two more are already modeled elsewhere (below).

Back to the Riffs index, where all seven are listed. Call counts and the cost ladder are estimates from how each activity is built, not measurements. The sharps content in the riffs is stand-in copy written for the experiments, not deck wording. Each riff runs with a live model call when it can reach the worker and falls back to a scripted stand-in when it can't. Add ?offline=1 to any riff to force the stand-in.

How the add-ons attach

Defend your call and Probe the miss aren't activities on their own. They sit on top of a rules-only activity the module already has, such as a hazard hunt or a pick-the-steps check. Each riff carries a tiny stand-in host so you can see the pair working.

  • Defend your call follows a choice. One sentence of reasoning separates a good guess from understanding.
  • Probe the miss follows a result. If the learner got something wrong, one question aims at that exact gap. A perfect run costs nothing.

Guardrails for anything that calls a model

  • Grade or generate only from the facts supplied for that item. The model never invents a rule.
  • Ask for JSON-only replies. Anything malformed or out of bounds falls back to the scripted stand-in.
  • Apply the non-answer policy: a question or "I don't know" is not a failed attempt.
  • Keep a hand-built fallback for when the worker is unreachable.
  • Draw from a pool of variants so a retry isn't a memorised answer.
