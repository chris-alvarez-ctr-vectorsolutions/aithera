Lesson Presentation Experiments — Accessibility Audit

A code-level review of all 25 prototypes in products/aithera/lesson-presentation — the 7 Scenario Simulator schemas (Conversation, Guided Arc, Branching Arc, Ensemble, Scene Sweep, Teach-Back, Scene Analysis) and the 11 lesson-step experiments. For each: what it uses (video, live AI, drag, TTS, voice, photo-tap…), the main compliance concerns, how hard each is to remediate, and what to change. Review tooling (toolbox, flow map, version switchers) is excluded.

Updated July 28, 2026 · Full HTML/CSS/JS read of every file, the shared js/sim-* simulation modules, the frame.js/frame.css course chrome, and css/lesson.css / js/lesson.js. Supersedes the July 7–8 audit (then 16 files).

What changed since the July 7–8 audit

This refresh does two things: it re-verifies the original 16 against current code, and it adds the 9 prototypes built since — the Live-AI Conversation cuts (action-practice-live-v3, story-highlight-practice-live), the Guided Arc family (guided-arc-live, marshall-live, marshall-live-v2), Teach-Back, and the three BETA POCs (Branching Arc, Ensemble, Scene Sweep).

Four of the six shared "fix once" items from last time are now cleared in code: the global reduced-motion block, the faint-ink contrast tokens, the fake radio-group pattern, and — the big one — the coach experiments now inject dialogue into genuine aria-live regions instead of silent containers. One of the two hard blockers is fixed too (Live AI's aria-hidden nav). New surface has appeared, mostly in the shared course chrome and the live-region render strategy — detailed below.

Scope decision — the prototype video player is out of scope

Per product direction, these prototypes' hand-rolled video player is a known limitation: a conformant player will be dropped in for production. So the whole media-player class of findings is parked and excluded from the rankings — it would otherwise dominate the analysis without telling us anything actionable about the prototype work.

Parked (assumed handled by the production player): prerecorded captions 1.2.2, transcript / media alternative 1.2.1 · 1.2.3, audio description 1.2.5, and caption timing that isn't synced to the pause button 2.2.1 · 2.2.2 — i.e. today's JS-timed caption chunks that keep advancing while the video is paused. Affects hazmat-scene-practice, action-practice (+ -live, -live-v3), marshall-live (+ -v2), guided-arc-live, and the two placeholder-video reflections when their real clips land.

Still in scope (a player swap does not fix these): not gating progression on watching / a skip path, moving focus onto the video screen when it appears, and a correct accessible name per clip. These stay as ordinary quick wins. Everything else below assumes the player problem is solved.

The headline

With the video player parked, the analysis stops being a video story and the real cost centers surface clearly. There are three. (1) Pointer-only mechanics that gate progression — text drag-highlighting and Scene Sweep's tap-the-photo hazard hunt have no keyboard path and hard-block keyboard/switch users; these are the only hard blockers left. (2) How the live conversation is rendered to a screen reader — the good news is the dialogue now lands in real aria-live regions (last audit's worst finding), but the scene region is torn down and rebuilt (innerHTML='') on every turn, so assistive tech re-reads the whole growing transcript each line instead of just the new one. (3) The new shared course chrome (frame.js), which now wraps ~24 pages and ships with no skip link, a duplicate <h1>, and a ⋯ menu whose ARIA doesn't match its behavior. Live AI, TTS, drag, voice — none of those modalities is inherently the problem; the render strategy and two input mechanics are.

The hard blockers — two pointer-only mechanics

Everything else degrades the experience; these prevent completion outright for keyboard and switch users. Both are the same shape: a pointer gesture is the only way to produce the thing a locked Continue button waits for. The last audit's third blocker — Live AI's aria-hidden navigation — is now fixed.

Drag-to-highlight gates the story screen Blocker

In both With story intro builds (scripted and Live AI), a highlight can only be created by drag-selecting text with a pointer (getSelection() on mouseup/touchend), and the Continue button stays disabled until one exists. Removal is now keyboard-operable and the count is announced — real progress — but a keyboard-only or switch user still can't make the first highlight, so they can't leave the screen. Needs an alternate selection model (per-sentence toggle marks). 2.1.1 · 2.5.7

story-highlight-practice.html · story-highlight-practice-live.html

Scene Sweep's hazards are click-only Blocker

In the HazCom POC, hazards are marked by tapping a full-bleed photo — a bare <div> reading pointer coordinates, with no focusable targets and no keyboard/arrow path. The "Talk it through" gate only opens after pointer marks or misses, so a keyboard user's only way forward is the mode toggle — landing in the coach with zero hazards found, the coverage rail never reached. The scenario already grounds to a written, zone-tagged scene, so a focusable hazard list that Enter-marks (reusing markHazard()) is a feasible additive fix. 2.1.1 · 2.5.1

scene-sweep-live.html

Fix once, fix (almost) everywhere

The shared-layer story has two halves now: what last audit's "fix once" items delivered, and the new shared surface introduced by the live-AI simulation stack and its course chrome.

Cleared since July — four of the old six systemic items

  • Reduced motion is now global. css/lesson.css carries a universal prefers-reduced-motion block that zeroes every animation/transition, and the JS-driven beats self-gate on a shared reducedMotion() check. 2.3.3
  • Faint-ink contrast is fixed at the token level. --ink-faint moved to #717a85 (≥4.5:1) with the old #9ca3af quarantined for decorative use; the sim pages darkened their own faint tokens to match. 1.4.3
  • Dialogue is announced. The coach experiments no longer inject conversation into silent containers — coach and scene both render into genuine aria-live regions with speaker attribution. This was last audit's single worst pattern. 4.1.3
  • One shared radio-group pattern. Quick Question / Peer Results / Peak BAC now have arrow-key + roving-tabindex behavior (shared Lesson.radiogroup() or inline equivalents), not role-without-behavior. 4.1.2 · 2.1.1

Residual from the old set: the white-on-teal button contrast is avoided in the sim components (they use an indigo accent ≈5:1) but lesson.css still defines the ~3.2:1 teal — keep white labels on --accent-strong, and switch the debrief primary button to it. Headings are now present everywhere, though the chrome introduces a duplicate <h1> (below).

1 · Live conversation region is rebuilt wholesale each turn

Every live sim page declares its scene stage (#stageInner) aria-live="polite" — then repaints it with innerHTML='' + a full re-render on every beat. A screen reader re-announces the entire growing transcript each line, not just the new one (worst in Ensemble, where multiple speakers make long bursts), and the rebuild drops keyboard focus off any in-scene button. The coach panel already renders incrementally (reconcileCoachBody) — that's the template. 4.1.3 · 2.4.3

All live sim pages · copy-pasted page shell

2 · The course chrome (frame.js) has three gaps

The production chrome now wraps ~24 pages and ships with: no skip link to the <main> it precedes 2.4.1; a duplicate <h1> (it injects its own lesson-title <h1> while each page already has one) inside plain <div>s with no banner/nav landmark 1.3.1; and a ⋯ menu declared role="menu" whose children are plain links, with aria-haspopup but no focus move into the sheet and no focus restore on close 4.1.2 · 2.4.3.

js/frame.js — every frame-wrapped page

3 · Progression state isn't announced

Branching Arc and Ensemble climb an escalation ladder and record a calibration tier per phase — but the level change and tier are written to state silently. A screen-reader user only perceives progress if the authored bridge copy happens to mention it. Add an explicit announce() on each phase/level transition. 4.1.3

branching-arc-live.html · ensemble-arc-live.html

4 · Residual focus & motion odds and ends

The shared debrief modal is exemplary via makeFlow (dialog role, background inert, focus in + restore, Escape) — but its numeric score count-up ignores reduced motion, and pages that bypass makeFlow (Teach-Back's score modal) lose the return-focus. Also shared-and-small: hover-only mode-toggle tooltips 1.4.13 and a composer whose aria-label stays "Your message" even when it retargets to a character 4.1.2 · 2.4.6. 2.3.3 · 2.4.3

Conversation Simulation · Story + highlight

story-highlight-practice.html · story-highlight-practice-live.htmlHigh
drag-select highlightingscripted & live AI roleplayfree-text inputmodalsno video/audio

Why High: the defining mechanic — free-range drag-to-highlight — has no keyboard equivalent and gates progression in both the scripted V1 and the Live-AI V2. Designing an accessible alternative (per-sentence toggle marks, "mark this passage" affordances) is real interaction-design work. Around it, the familiar shared items apply.

Main concerns

  • Highlights can only be created by pointer drag; Continue is disabled until one exists — a hard keyboard/switch blocker. 2.1.1 · 2.5.7
  • Each <mark>'s aria-label="Highlighted — press to remove" overrides the wrapped text, so AT users hear the label, not what was highlighted. 4.1.2 · 1.3.1
  • The passage lives inside the live region and is rebuilt on every highlight change → the whole story can re-announce on each add/remove (systemic #1, amplified here). 4.1.3
  • (Live V2) the intro "how to highlight" dialog has no focus restore or Escape; the results modal lacks the Escape the V3 sibling has. 2.4.3

Quick wins

  • Include the highlighted text in each mark's accessible name; announce when Continue flips enabled; add the intro-modal focus-restore + results-modal Escape

Structural

  • A keyboard / single-pointer selection model (focusable sentences toggled with Enter/Space — it mirrors the removal path that already works)

Already good: highlight removal is fully keyboard-operable with post-removal focus management; count changes are announced; the results modal uses the shared focus/inert/restore flow.

Quick wins

Structural

Already good: the photo mounts once (so it doesn't re-announce on every render), the coverage counter is live, pin animations are reduced-motion gated, and the coach chat rides the strong shared shell.
