Writer Studio · a guide for the design team

You're not writing answer keys anymore. You're writing guidance.

In a multiple-choice item, you spell out every path a learner can take. In an AI conversation, you can't — the learner types anything, and the AI improvises a response. Your job shifts from scripting every answer to setting the structure the AI performs inside. Writer Studio is where you do that, in plain-language fields — never raw prompt text.

The good news: your craft carries over. Distractors become "what a weak answer looks like." Per-answer feedback becomes a character's believable reaction. Teaching points become a guaranteed takeaway. The studio labels each field with the old-craft equivalent as you go.

Step one: pick the kind of practice

Writer Studio isn't one form — it's a form that changes based on the type of practice you're building. Each type is a different way to assess, so it asks for different things. Two are available today.

Action Practice

Rehearse a hard conversation

The learner talks to a simulated person while an AI coach steps in and out to reflect with them. Scoring is silent — the point is the conversation.

Reach for this when the skill is what you say and do — an intervention, a difficult feedback talk, a de-escalation.
  • The situation The scene as it stands when the learner walks in. The AI treats every detail as true — this is your question stem.
  • Assessment dimensions What each line is judged on: what "strong" looks like, and what "weak" looks like — your right answer and your distractors.
  • Character reactions If the learner does X, the character does Y — this replaces per-answer feedback.
  • The gate The one observable action that must happen before the scene can end well — your passing criterion.
  • The playbook The takeaways every learner leaves with, guaranteed — your SME-validated teaching points.
Teach-Back

Explain it back from memory

The learner teaches a set of required topics in their own words. Blurred tiles come into focus as they cover each one, and the AI grades coverage live. Here the score is the point.

Reach for this when the skill is recall and understanding — can they explain what a complete program must cover?
  • Required topics The checklist a complete answer must cover. Each has a short label, a full description the AI grades against, and accepted phrasings — so plain language still earns credit.
  • Calibration chat A short, no-scoring warm-up before the tiles appear, to settle the learner in.
  • Grading rules The judgment calls: what counts, what doesn't, which topics are easy to confuse.
  • Closing feedback How the results are phrased — warm and non-punitive ("you named 7 of 10," never "you missed 3").

What's always handled for you

No matter which type you're authoring, the studio takes care of the parts that would be risky to hand-write. You never touch these — they're built in.

Safety guardrails are locked

The rules that keep the AI safe — how it handles trolling, what to do if a learner discloses a real crisis, the exact output shape — ship inside every scenario. You can read them; you can't break them.

The Guardrails tab checks your work

As you type, it flags gaps like a spellcheck for scenarios. Warnings are advice; blocking issues stop you from publishing something broken.

Your work saves itself

Drafts autosave. Save named versions to your Library, and Export or Import JSON to hand a scenario to a colleague.

See exactly what the AI reads

The Compiled Prompt tab shows the full instructions your fields produce, with your words highlighted — so there's no black box.

The rhythm of authoring

01

Fill the fields

Describe the situation, the answer key, and the takeaways — in plain language.

02

Clear the guardrails

Work down the flags until nothing is blocking.

03

Publish

Send your draft to the learner prototype.

04

Preview & refine

Open the learner preview, run it yourself, and come back to adjust.

One habit worth forming early: in Action Practice, write names as {{learner}} and {{character}} instead of typing them out. Change the name once and it updates everywhere.
