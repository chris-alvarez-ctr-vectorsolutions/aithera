Writer Studio · a guide for the design team

You're not writing answer keys anymore. You're writing guidance.

In a multiple-choice item, you spell out every path a learner can take. In an AI conversation, you can't — the learner types anything, and the AI improvises a response. Your job shifts from scripting every answer to setting the structure the AI performs inside. Writer Studio is where you do that, in plain-language fields — never raw prompt text.

How you author here

A situation, an answer key, and guardrails

You describe what "good" looks like and what must happen. The AI plays it out live — differently for every learner.

The good news: your craft carries over. Distractors become "what a weak answer looks like." Per-answer feedback becomes a believable reaction. Teaching points become a guaranteed takeaway. The studio labels each field with the old-craft equivalent as you go.

One simulator, three sections

Every scenario you build — whatever the mode — has the same three-section spine. It's the same spine the live experience runs on, so what you author maps one-to-one onto what a learner walks through. The studio's left nav is grouped by these three sections.

①

Scenario Context

The swappable intro that sets the scene — a video cold open, narrated audio, a reading, a story the learner highlights, or nothing at all. It hands the learner into the loop.

②

Interaction

The live loop — this is where the difficulty lives, and the one section the mode reshapes.

ENTER→ (ACT·REACT·COACH)×N ↺GATE→ EXIT
③

Debrief & Close

Analyze → Results → Takeaways. The guaranteed, expert-validated playbook and resources every learner leaves with — authored by you, identical for everyone, however their loop went.

Inside Section ②, some beats are LOCKED (the app owns them and delivers them verbatim — the safety engine, and in some modes the exact prompts) and some are DYNAMIC (the AI improvises from your fields). The Compiled Prompt tab highlights the dynamic parts so you always see which is which.

Step one: pick a mode

A mode is a way of assessing — and it's the only thing that changes Section ②. Context and Debrief work the same across all of them. Pick the mode by the skill you're after; the studio then asks for exactly what that shape needs. Four are available today.

Roleplay

Rehearse a hard conversation

The learner talks to a simulated person while an AI coach steps in and out to reflect with them. Scoring is silent — the point is the conversation.

Reach for this when the skill is what you say and do — an intervention, a difficult feedback talk, a de-escalation.
ACT→REACT→COACH→GATE Say a line → the character reacts → the coach reflects → a gate holds until the key move lands.

Example: intervening for a friend (the Kendra scenario).

Guided Arc

Walk a scripted arc, then reflect

The coach walks the learner through authored beats it delivers word-for-word — a reflection, a question that has a right answer — then the learner observes a short video and reflects on what they'd do. One coach follow-up lands it. No role-play.

Reach for this when there's specific content to land (a legal standard, a policy) plus a moment worth reflecting on.
COACH→ACT→COACH→EXIT You author the LOCKED beats verbatim; the AI writes only the calibrated feedback around them.

Example: bystander intervention (the Marshall scenario).

Observe / React

Review footage, one beat at a time

The learner reviews footage in segments — often a "what not to do." Between clips the coach probes one beat at a time: it asks first, then validates or corrects. It ends on a synthesis. No role-play.

Reach for this when the skill is reading a situation — a scene size-up, spotting the hazard, noticing what's wrong.
ACT→REACT→GATE→EXIT Watch a segment → answer the coach → nudge-then-advance → a final synthesis completes it.

Example: hazmat scene size-up (the I-65 tanker rollover).

Teach-Back

Explain it back from memory

The learner teaches a set of required topics in their own words. Blurred tiles come into focus as they cover each one, and the AI grades coverage live. Here the score is the point.

Reach for this when the skill is recall and understanding — can they explain what a complete program must cover?
ENTER→ACT→REACT→RESULTS A warm-up, then teach each topic → tiles focus and the grader nudges toward gaps → the score is shown.

Example: applying HazCom (the Teach Me exercise).

What's always handled for you

No matter which mode you're authoring, the studio takes care of the parts that would be risky to hand-write. You never touch these — they're built in.

Safety guardrails are locked

The rules that keep the AI safe — how it handles trolling, what to do if a learner discloses a real crisis, the exact output shape — ship inside every scenario. You can read them; you can't break them.

The Guardrails tab checks your work

As you type, it flags gaps like a spellcheck for scenarios. Warnings are advice; blocking issues stop you from publishing something broken.

Your work saves itself

Drafts autosave. Save named versions to your Library, and Export or Import JSON to hand a scenario to a colleague.

See exactly what the AI reads

The Compiled Prompt tab shows the full instructions your fields produce, with your dynamic words highlighted against the locked engine — so there's no black box.

One habit worth forming early: in the conversation modes, write names as {{learner}} and {{character}} instead of typing them out. Change the name once and it updates everywhere.
