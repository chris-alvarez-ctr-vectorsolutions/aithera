v4 supersedes the walkthrough screenshots read first

The earlier call captures were an older draft. v4 moved three things, and each moves toward what we already recommended:

  • Terminology: the draft's stage (kind interact/evaluate) is gone. A phase is now exactly { practice, debrief } — practice is where the learner acts, debrief is its own first-class unit.
  • Version + root: schema_version is "4.0"; the document root is { implementation_id, modality, schema_version, content }. The draft's learning_objective/implementations[]/is_default wrapper is removed — LO identity now lives in "the surrounding content system" (§F).
  • No scenario type field: §1.1 states it outright — what a scenario is emerges from its parts. That is our runtime schema's x-discriminator, verbatim in spirit.
§A · The map

Their practice is our rung; their phase is a layer above

The v4 phase is a story segment shown to the learner as "Phase 2 of 3" — a grouping of exactly one practice and one debrief. Our ladder has no such grouping (our level is a cosmetic label). Their practice is the turn-owning rung — that's our phase. Their debrief is a second rung we express as fields today (§C-2).

Their §2 runtime contract IS our conformance guarantees

Advancement is server-owned and forward-only; the model's [[phase:…]] marker is advisory (our action/tier). The never-dangle rule. Prompt scoping is "discipline, not secrecy." Internal assessment is trace-only. The close fires 100%, verified piece-by-piece against the authored ideal_response. Two independent specs landed on the same invariants — the strongest possible confirmation the shared runtime is the right shape.

The 4-layer canon model (§4.2) ↔ our engine

1  Global canon        (time-invariant world truth)   → scene_world.canon.facts[]   ≈ our canon[]
2  Story events         (happens later in the arc)     → the locked opener of that unit  ≈ our entry.beats
3  Learner aftermath    (differs per learner)          → carryover transcript          ≈ our transitions[].set  (but verbatim, not a summary — §C-3)
4  Secrets              (character-held, earned)        → characters[].canon_facts.reveal_when ≈ our ensemble disclosures
§C · What genuinely differs

Four sharper calls — three worth adopting, one a decision

None of these is us being coarser. Each is a place v4 committed to a stricter choice than our reference did.

1

Prompt isolation via two threaded conversations. they're ahead

v4 runs one continuous coach conversation plus short-lived scenes, carried as threaded {coach, practice} histories keyed by phase id. A practice prompt never contains teaching_points, misconceptions, a debrief's key_points, or ideal_response — those reach the coach only on debrief/closing turns, and ideal_response reaches no prompt at all (composed server-side). Even mid-scene help is scope-clean and budget-inert. We run one static compiled prompt for the whole run and keep teaching out of scenes by instruction.

Consider adopting. It's a stronger anti-leak guarantee than ours. It's an engine change, and it means our promptHash can't equal theirs by construction — conformance (§G) must compare per-scope, not per-run.

2

The debrief is a first-class unit; ours is two fields.

A v4 debrief ({phase_id}.debrief) owns turns via follow_up_turns (0 = delivery-only, the default; ≥1 = interactive), carries key_points (statements to land regardless), its own levels grading the practice attempt, an optional locked probe (verbatim question + its own levels grading answers), and a final_word. Ours is talkItThrough + points, delivered as one teach turn.

Low-cost to align, and it unlocks #1. Promoting our debrief to its own rung is a normalize-time transform. The independently-scopeable debrief is exactly what makes the two-conversation model buildable.

3

Cross-scene memory is a verbatim transcript, not a summary. they're ahead

Their carryover grants a later scene the earlier practice's verbatim transcript — explicitly "never a summary, because summarising is where a model starts inventing." Our transitions[].set writes exactly that summary: a disposition string like "steadied — heard, with limits he accepted". Same goal (their canon layer 3: learner-dependent aftermath); they deliberately rejected the mechanism we use.

Consider adopting. Since no shipped scenario routes on tier (every transitions[] converges on one next, varying only set), our whole use of set is this carry-forward — which v4 does better with a transcript. Swapping the disposition summary for a carryover transcript is a clean upgrade.

4

A fixed 3-level scale vs our open tier vocabulary. decision

v4 fixes the quality scale at three — unthoughtful / neutral / strong — set by the engine's templates; the author writes only each level's look_for / response. Our calibration[].tier is author-defined per phase: mix-arc ships CONNECTS / VAGUE / CONFRONTS. A scenario with custom tier labels does not port to their fixed set.

Our call to make. Our own contract already warns against a second tier list; their fixed-3 is the strict form of that discipline. Either normalize our custom tiers onto the three, or make the case to them for an extensible scale. Not a blocker either way — but it decides whether a mix-arc export loads unchanged.

Nuances worth noting, not acting on

  • Content-only, lint-enforced. v4 content carries zero prompt text; a prompt-smell lint rejects strings like "the ai will" or "the model does not". Some of our authored guidance ("CALIBRATION ONLY, do not evaluate…") would fail it — a stricter data/prompt separation than ours.
  • World split by audience. narrative is the coach's ground truth; scene_world is the scenes'. The coach never sees scene_world; scenes never see narrative. We keep one world scope.
  • Reactions factored per-practice. Character cards hold identity only; how a character reacts lives in each practice's levels "because reactions differ per scene." We bundle reactions on the cast card.
  • Provenance points outward. source_references are external authorities only (OSHA, Title VII) — never an internal course/slide id. Our playbook[].source cites internal objects (RVCT-479 P017) — a different, audit-facing purpose; don't conflate them.
§D · The layers

Both sides think in the same three layers

v4's design constraint 1 — "Content-only. Scenario data carries no prompt text; deterministic serializers render prompts" — is our authored-source → toRuntime / compile split, stated as a hard rule and enforced by a lint. Their content is one authored document, but §2 shows it reaches the model in scoped slices (teaching withheld mid-attempt, released at debrief). The seam between authored data and rendered prompt is real on both sides; v4 simply polices it harder than we do.

§E · The envelope

LO identity moved out — exactly where we said it belonged

The draft's learning_objective / implementations[] / is_default wrapper is gone from v4. The document is now one implementation; §3 states "LO-level identity, revisioning, and catalog metadata belong to the surrounding content system, which selects an implementation and hands it to this service." So the boundary we drew is now their architecture: our scenario object is their content, and the only thing above it in the document is a thin root.

v4 document root — and the matching passthrough in normalize()

// v4 root — LO/implementations[] no longer here; that's the content system's
{ "implementation_id": "…", "modality": "ai-conversational", "schema_version": "4.0", "content": { … } }

// optional self-describing export — engine ignores it, export preserves it
source.meta = source.meta || {};
source.meta.implementation_id = source.meta.implementation_id || "";
source.meta.modality          = "ai-conversational";
source.meta.schema_version    = source.meta.schema_version || "4.0";

The one genuine design question the envelope still raises is the picker UX — when a learner or admin chooses among implementations of one objective — which marshall-mode-select already prototypes (AI vs no-AI = two implementations of one LO). That's a mock, not an engine change.

§F · Conformance

Proving the two engines agree

Once the port has toRuntime / compile equivalents, agreement is a hash: js/sim-snapshot.js emits a runtimeHash + promptHash; same source, both engines, equal hashes ⇒ they conform.

One caveat from §C-1: v4's two-conversation scoping means the model sees a different prompt at a scene turn than at a debrief turn, so a single whole-run promptHash stops being meaningful. If we adopt scoping, conformance must hash per-scope — the coach-conversation prompt and each scene prompt separately. Worth agreeing on snapshot granularity before either side hardens it.

§G · Decisions

What's actually on us now

No blockers remain — the spec is complete. What's left are four choices, all ours to make deliberately:

  • Adopt two-conversation prompt scoping? (§C-1.) The one place they're clearly ahead; it changes what conformance means.
  • Adopt verbatim carryover over our disposition summary? (§C-3.) A clean upgrade — our set is only ever used as carry-forward, which a transcript does better.
  • Promote the debrief to a first-class unit? (§C-2.) Low-cost, and the enabler for scoping.
  • Fixed 3-level scale, or keep our open tier vocabulary? (§C-4.) Decides whether a custom-tier mix-arc export loads unchanged.

The envelope (§E) needs nothing from us; the identity passthrough is optional. My lean: hold all engine changes and take #1–#3 as one coordinated pass, since they touch the same code — then settle #4 with the dev team rather than in the engine.
