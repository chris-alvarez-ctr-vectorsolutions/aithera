DANA: [curious] If I'm wearing thick gloves, do I even need a sharps container?

RAY: Well, it's a fair question, so let's make it a real choice. Suppose a plant wants to protect its people, but it can only do one thing. It can hand out heavier gloves, or it can put sharps containers on every line.

DANA: I think I'd pick the gloves. They're right there on your hands.

RAY: That makes sense, and heavy-duty gloves do help. They protect you against cuts and punctures.

DANA: [playful] I can hear a "but" coming.

RAY: [matter-of-fact] The catch is that a needle can get through EVEN a thick glove. Imagine a needle tip pressed against a heavy work glove, and it can still push through.

DANA: [surprised] Huh. I kind of assumed a thick glove would stop just about anything.

DANA: So what does the container do that the glove can't?

RAY: A safety-engineered sharps container is what's called an engineering control. That means it works on the sharp itself, because once the sharp drops in, the container holds it inside.

DANA: So the gloves are on me, and the container is on the SHARP.

RAY: That's really the difference.

DANA: So is the glove enough on its own?

RAY: No. The gloves still help, but on their own they give you less protection.

DANA: Okay. That changes my answer.
