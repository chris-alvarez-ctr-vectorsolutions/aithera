DANA: Here's a quick one about a used blade. Say you've just finished a blade change, and the old blade is still in your hand. What do you do with it?

RAY: Well, you might want to set it down for a minute, or drop it in the nearest trash can. But NEITHER one is safe.

DANA: [skeptical] Why not, if it's only for a minute?

RAY: [matter-of-fact] Because a used blade is a hazard even when it looks clean. The next person who reaches for that spot, or that bag, is at risk.

DANA: So what's the right way to handle it?

RAY: There are four steps. One, know your route to a sharps container before you start. Two, use a safer tool if one will do the job. Three, turn on the safety feature if there is one. Four, put the used blade straight into a designated sharps container.

DANA: [playful] I guess that sounds simple enough. Does it change if the container is far away?

RAY: No, it doesn't. Actually, the whole rule comes down to this: a used blade goes STRAIGHT into the container.
