DANA: Ray, when we were setting up, you brought up used blades left sitting out. Why do those stick with you?

RAY: [thoughtful] Honestly, because a used blade is a sharp, just like a needle. It just doesn't look like one.

DANA: I always picture needles. So what counts as a sharp?

RAY: A sharp is ANYTHING that can puncture or cut the skin, so a used blade counts. Needles still show up too, because some people manage diabetes or allergies with injections.

DANA: Right. So how does a blade end up sitting out?

RAY: Say you've just changed a blade, but the sharps container is over by the other station. So you set the old one on top of your toolbox and think, "I'll come right back for it."

DANA: [playful] That sounds kind of like something I'd do. What's the harm if you come right back?

RAY: [matter-of-fact] Somebody else might reach for that spot. A used blade is a hazard whether or not you can see anything on it.

DANA: Okay, back up. Say it's still in your hand. What do you do?

RAY: You take it to the container. And actually, you plan for this before you pick up the blade.

DANA: How so?

RAY: There are four steps. One, know your route to the container before you begin. Two, use a safer tool if one will do the job. Three, turn on the safety feature if there is one. Four, put the blade directly into a designated sharps container.

DANA: It doesn't go on the toolbox. What about the regular trash?

RAY: That's out too.

RAY: A used blade goes STRAIGHT into the container, every time.

DANA: Got it.

RAY: [warmly] What I tell crews: don't turn that blade into somebody else's problem.
