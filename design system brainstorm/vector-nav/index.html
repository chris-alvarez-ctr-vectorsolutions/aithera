Atlas navigation shell · 6 explorations

One shell, many products: each version wraps the same neutral placeholder suite (Learn · Comply · Schedule, standing in for real Vector products) and varies exactly one thing at a time: the app switcher, the side-nav hierarchy, density/collapse, and whether the product's modes live in top tabs or in the side nav. All six share identical design tokens, so differences you see are structure, not styling.

Fixed in every version: the shared shell contract
Nav open/close App switcher Product · Org Search ⌘K + New Alerts Help Avatar
The nav open/close control is always the far-left item, directly above the side nav's edge; the app switcher sits next to it and owns that corner in every product. Switching ≠ navigating: other products never appear inside the current product's nav tree, and universal actions never move. Only the middle (mode tabs or nothing) and the side-nav pattern vary per version.
Menus borrow two things from Convergence + EHS (the visual style stays this exploration's own): the disclosure chevron trails the label and flips when open, as in .sn-row / .sb-item, so an expandable row and a plain row at the same level share one icon x and one label x; and the menu vocabulary echoes the real products' IA: Organization / Teams / Departments, Curricula, Qualifications, Activities, My recents / Scheduled reports on the Learn side; Incidents summary, Investigations, Corrective actions, OSHA logs, and the Safety Management long tail (Hazards, JSA, SDS, Tasks, Events, Document library, Observations) on the Comply side.

V1 · Grid launcher + mode tabs in the top bar

Switcher: waffle launcher Nav: accordion Tabs: in top bar Light

The corner waffle launcher opens the design-system app switcher (product rows with logo, name and open-in-new, plus an unlicensed row), and it WORKS: pick Comply and the shell re-skins. Learn's menus are the real Convergence NAV_TREE (Admin mode carries it in full, Dashboard through System); Comply's are the real EHS sidebar, with Safety management as a section and only EHS's actual sub-groups (Inspections, Observations, Risk management, Industrial hygiene) as accordions. On Learn, the mode tabs live-swap the side nav: tabs = mode, side nav = within-mode. Trade-off: an anonymous waffle icon hides which apps you own, and long tab labels compete with search for width.

V2 · App rail + two-pane nav, no tabs

Switcher: left icon rail Nav: two-pane Tabs: none Light

Slack/Teams pattern: the suite is always visible in a dark far-left rail, so switching is one click and physically separate from navigating. The product nav is two panes (icon sections, then the selected section's items), and there is no tab row: the whole structure lives on the left. Click the rail products to feel the "one shell, many products" swap. Trade-off: three chrome columns (~366px) before content starts, and the rail only scales to a handful of products.

V3 · Sidebar product dropdown + 3-level tree

Switcher: sidebar dropdown Nav: nested tree · 3 levels Tabs: none Light

Notion/Linear pattern: the current product's name is the sidebar header; open it to switch. Most chrome-efficient switcher of the set, and the named control tells you where you are. This version carries the full hierarchy requirement, Training → Compliance courses → OSHA 10 · 2024, with depth signaled purely by indentation and guide lines, capped at three levels. Trade-off: deep trees demand discipline, and the switcher is less discoverable than a dedicated corner control.

V4 · Dense dark: collapsed rail + flyout submenus

Switcher: waffle launcher Nav: icon rail + flyouts Tabs: none Dark · compact

Collapse as a designed state: the product nav is a 56px icon rail; sections with children open flyout panels on hover and keyboard focus (the "flyout open" link pins the Shifts flyout for review). Compact metrics (48px bar, 28px items) leave maximum room for the scheduling board, and the dark theme proves the token contract re-skins without touching a component rule. Trade-off: icon-only nav taxes recognition until icons are learned; flyouts hide siblings.

V5 · Top-bar product switcher + dedicated tab row

Switcher: named dropdown Nav: shallow flat list Tabs: own row Light

The closest analog to Convergence/Salesforce: a named product dropdown top-left (always spelled out, unlike V1's anonymous grid) and a dedicated second row of mode tabs with room to breathe. With tabs carrying the big modes, the side nav within a mode stays a flat list plus saved views. Compare directly with V1: same tabs philosophy, two rows vs one. Trade-off: ~100px of stacked chrome before content, and two horizontal layers plus a side nav gives three places to look.

V6 · Synthesis (recommended)

Switcher: left icon rail Nav: flat + collapse-to-rail Tabs: per-product optional Light

The strongest pieces combined: V2's always-visible app rail (switching gets a physical answer), V1/V5's mode tabs as an opt-in layer (Learn and Comply have tab rows, Schedule opts out and the shell still reads as one system), and a side nav whose header names the product and collapses to an icon rail with tooltip labels (auto-collapses below 1120px; resize to see the responsive state). Depth inside a mode is capped at two because tabs absorb one level.

Thumbnails are live, scaled iframes of the actual files. If your browser blocks them when opening from disk, use the Open buttons: every file is self-contained. Once inside a version, the dark pill in the bottom-left corner jumps straight to any other version (review tooling, not part of the design).

Where this lands

Advance V6, pressure-tested against V5. The app rail is the only switcher that makes the suite permanently visible while keeping other products out of the current product's nav tree, and "tabs as an opt-in per-product layer" lets deep products (Convergence-like) and shallow ones share a single shell without forcing empty chrome on the simple case. If the suite grows past 5 or 6 products, the rail stops scaling and V5's named dropdown becomes the fallback switcher, which is why it stays in the comparison set.

Top three open questions for the Vector team:

  1. How many products does a typical org license? The rail is great at 3, wrong at 10; the answer picks the switcher.
  2. Do Vector's deep products need level-3 nav items (V3) once tabs absorb the mode level, or is 2 levels + tabs always enough?
  3. Who owns the tab row per product: a fixed platform contract, or can product teams add/remove modes freely (and if so, how is drift prevented)?
